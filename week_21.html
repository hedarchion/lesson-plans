Monday DLP — Vocabulary & Match

I1D • 2:20–3:20 PM • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Greet students and ask: "Where did you go last weekend?"<br>2. Show a picture of a busy market — ask "What do you see? What do you hear?"<br>3. Elicit 3-4 responses, write key words on board (market, food, people).</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Places &amp; Activities Vocabulary</strong></p>
<p>1. Introduce 6 places with picture cards: market, library, park, cinema, mosque, restaurant<br>2. Say each word clearly — students repeat chorally then individually<br>3. For each place, model 1 activity: "At the market, I buy food"</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Match &amp; Say (Pairs)</strong></p>
<p>1. Give each pair a set of picture cards and word cards<br>2. Students match picture → word, then practice saying: "This is the [place]"<br>3. Teacher walks around, checks pronunciation, gives feedback<br>4. 3 pairs present 2 matched places each to the class</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Quick recall: hold up a picture, class says the place name together<br>2. Exit ticket: each student writes 1 place name and 1 activity on a slip of paper</p>

Reflection

Picture activity engaged most students well. Dharshayani, Thaqif Umar and Nur Faqihah Humaira volunteered answers confidently. Tishanthan and Tharinish needed extra prompting to repeat words — will sit closer on Tuesday. Overall good first session.

Tuesday DLP — Sentence Building

S1D • 3:50–4:50 PM • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Flash 6 place pictures from Monday — students call out names as review<br>2. Write the sentence frame on the board: "I go to the ___ to ___"<br>3. Model with 2 examples: "I go to the market to buy food." / "I go to the park to play."</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Building Sentences</strong></p>
<p>1. Drill the sentence frame: whole class → groups → individual volunteers<br>2. Add 3 more activities: read books (library), watch a film (cinema), pray (mosque), eat (restaurant)<br>3. Write 2 complete sentences on the board as models, underline the place and activity words</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Fill-in-the-Blanks (Individual → Pair)</strong></p>
<p>1. Give worksheet with 6 incomplete sentences: "I go to the ___ to ___"<br>2. Students fill in blanks using word bank at the top of the page<br>3. In pairs, students read their completed sentences to each other<br>4. Teacher checks 2-3 worksheets on the spot and gives feedback</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Random pick: 4 students read their best sentence to class<br>2. Class repeats the sentence in chorus</p>

Reflection

Good retention from Monday — most students recalled 5 of 6 place names in the warm-up. Fill-in-blanks was completed well by majority. Riszuan Haiqal and WARDAH needed extra help reading the word bank. Sentence frame drilling was effective.

Monday DLP — Describing Places in Detail

I1E • 4:50–5:50 PM • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Think-Pair-Share: "Where did you go last weekend? What did you do there?"<br>2. Students think 30s, share with partner 1 min, then 3-4 share with class<br>3. Write 3 sample answers on board; introduce topic: Describing places and activities.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Describing Places with Full Sentences</strong></p>
<p>1. Present 8 places: market, library, park, shopping mall, cinema, mosque, stadium, beach<br>2. For each place, model 2-3 activities in full present simple sentences<br>3. Show example: "At the shopping mall, I meet my friends, buy clothes and eat at the food court."<br>4. Highlight: WHO + GO WHERE + DO WHAT structure</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Pair Interview</strong></p>
<p>1. Give each pair a simple interview table: Name | Place | Activity 1 | Activity 2<br>2. Students interview 3 classmates: "Where do you go on weekends? What do you do there?"<br>3. Record answers in the table<br>4. Each student presents findings about 1 classmate to the class in full sentences</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Class analysis: "Which place was most popular in our class?"<br>2. Students respond in complete sentences: "Most people go to the ___."</p>

Reflection

Pair interview activity worked well — Celine Phee and Chai Zi Jian produced excellent full sentences. Kalvin Pillai and Keerthikan needed prompts to move beyond 1-word answers. The interview table provided good structure for weaker students. Most completed 3 interviews.

Wednesday DLP — Reading: Weekend Routines

R1E • 3:50–4:50 PM • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1, 3.1.2 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Show 3 pictures: a family at the park, a teenager at the cinema, a person at the library<br>2. Ask: "What can you see? What are they doing?" — brainstorm in pairs for 2 min<br>3. Elicit vocabulary: relax, enjoy, borrow, queue, picnic</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Reading Strategies: Main Ideas &amp; Details</strong></p>
<p>1. Distribute short reading text: "My Family's Weekend Routine" (4 short paragraphs)<br>2. Teach skimming: read the first and last sentence of each paragraph<br>3. Teach scanning: look for keywords (days, places, activities)<br>4. Read paragraph 1 aloud — students follow, underline 3 key words</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Comprehension Questions</strong></p>
<p>1. Students read the full text silently (5 min)<br>2. In pairs, answer 8 comprehension questions: 4 main-idea questions + 4 detail questions<br>3. Class discussion: check answers together, clarify misunderstandings<br>4. Extension for fast finishers: write 2 more questions about the text for a partner</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. 3-2-1 exit: Write 3 new words, 2 things you learned about the family, 1 question you still have</p>

Reflection

Reading comprehension was solid — Nurin Mirza and Low Mei Yik scored 8/8 on questions. Skimming/scanning strategy needs more practice; Adzin Danish and Khayla Dhia relied on reading every word. The 3-2-1 exit provided good insight into vocabulary gaps. 📊 Marks (Page 32): avg 9.0/12 (75%).

Thursday DLP — Writing: My Favourite Place

K1E • 3:50–4:50 PM • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.1, 4.1.5 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Show a model paragraph on board: "My Favourite Place" (5 sentences)<br>2. Read aloud together, ask: "What information does the writer include?"<br>3. Annotate on board: Place name → Activities → Who goes with → Feelings</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Paragraph Structure &amp; Connectors</strong></p>
<p>1. Teach 2-paragraph structure: Para 1 = describe the place + what you do | Para 2 = who goes with you + how you feel<br>2. Introduce connectors: and, also, because<br>3. Show example sentences with and without connectors — discuss which sounds better<br>4. Provide sentence starters bank: My favourite place is... / I go there... / I like it because...</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Draft, Feedback, Revise</strong></p>
<p>1. Students write a first draft of 2 paragraphs about their favourite place (10 min)<br>2. Peer feedback: swap with partner, check for 3 things — clear place name? activities listed? connectors used?<br>3. Students revise their draft based on partner feedback<br>4. 3 volunteers read final version aloud</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Gallery walk: leave paragraphs on desks, students walk around and read 3 others' work<br>2. Thumbs up/down: "Was writing in English easier today than Monday?"</p>

Reflection

Paragraphs showed good vocabulary from the week. Sharveni and Putri Ayesha produced excellent connected paragraphs with 'because' and 'also'. Nur Anis and Muhammad Ammar needed more scaffolding — sentence starters helped. Draft-feedback cycle was productive. 📊 Marks (W19-K Writing): avg 4.0/6 (67%).

Tuesday DLP — Extended Descriptions with Connectors

S1M • 5:50–6:50 PM • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. "I-spy" warm-up: "I spy a place where people borrow books and study quietly" → students guess (library)<br>2. Do 3 rounds with different places (market, cinema, park)<br>3. Reveal topic: Describing places with detail and reasons.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Using Connectors for Extended Descriptions</strong></p>
<p>1. Present 8 places with 3+ activities per place, showing them in a table on the board<br>2. Teach 4 connectors: and, also, because, so<br>3. Model 3 complex sentences: "I usually go to the library because it's quiet and I can read" / "The park has a playground and a jogging track, so many families go there"<br>4. Students identify the connector in each sentence and explain its function</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Sentence Combining (Pairs)</strong></p>
<p>1. Give pairs 8 short sentence pairs to combine using connectors (e.g., "The market is crowded. I like going there." → "The market is crowded but I like going there.")<br>2. Pairs write combined sentences on mini whiteboards<br>3. Hold up boards, class reads selected answers, teacher gives feedback<br>4. Each pair creates 2 original extended sentences about places they like</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Each student writes their best extended-description sentence on a sticky note<br>2. Stick on the "Place Descriptions" wall — class reads 3 before leaving</p>

Reflection

Strong start — Aayra Faiz and Gan Kok Xian used 'because' and 'also' naturally from the start. Thomawaty and Yaazhiniy were engaged throughout and produced good combined sentences. Sentence combining activity worked better than expected; mini whiteboards kept the pace fast. 'So' as a connector needs more practice.

Wednesday DLP — Describe & Guess Game

R1M • 5:50–6:50 PM • CS 2.4 / LS 2.4.1 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Review connectors from Tuesday: flash card with a connector word → students give an example sentence<br>2. "Mystery Place" demo: teacher describes a place using 3 clues, students guess<br>3. Elicit: "What made the description easy or hard to guess?"</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — How to Give Effective Clues</strong></p>
<p>1. Teach the 3-clue formula: Clue 1 = what kind of place | Clue 2 = what people do there | Clue 3 = a special detail<br>2. Show examples: "This is an outdoor place. People exercise and play sports here. You can see a big field and goalposts." → (stadium/sports complex)<br>3. Analyse each clue: which words helped you guess? Emphasise descriptive adjectives</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Describe &amp; Guess Tournament</strong></p>
<p>1. Each student secretly picks a place card (8 options)<br>2. Round 1: In pairs, Student A describes (3 clues using connectors), Student B guesses. Switch.<br>3. Round 2: Winners face new partners — describe 2 places this time<br>4. Final round: top 4 describers present to the class — class votes on best description</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Reflection: "What makes a good description?" — 3 ideas on the board from student suggestions<br>2. Preview Thursday: "Tomorrow you'll write a full description!"</p>

Reflection

Tournament format was very engaging — even usually quiet students like Choye Kai En and Look Tin Woun participated actively. The 3-clue formula gave clear structure. Ahmad Harraz and Muhammad Izzat produced excellent descriptions. One issue: some students forgot connectors in the excitement — will reinforce in writing tomorrow. 📊 Marks (Page 10): avg 7.9/10 (79%).

Tuesday DLP — Giving Reasons for Places

S2E • 2:20–3:20 PM • CS 2.1, 2.4 / LS 2.1.1, 2.4.1 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Think-Pair-Share: "What\'s your favourite place to go after school? Why?"<br>2. Students think 30s → share with partner 1 min → 4 share with class<br>3. Write key reasons on board: relaxing, fun, meet friends, quiet, exciting</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Giving Reasons with 'because'</strong></p>
<p>1. Present 8 places with 2-3 reasons each: park (relaxing, fresh air, exercise) / cinema (exciting, big screen, popcorn) / library (quiet, study, free) / mall (shopping, food, meet friends)<br>2. Model opinion sentences: "I prefer the park because it\'s relaxing and I can meet friends there"<br>3. Introduce comparative phrases: more... than, better than, not as... as</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Reason Stations (Group Rotation)</strong></p>
<p>1. Set up 4 stations around the room, each with a place picture + prompt card<br>2. In groups of 4-5, students rotate to each station (3 min each)<br>3. At each station, each student says 1 reason they like/dislike that place using "because"<br>4. Group scribe notes the best reason for each station<br>5. Each group presents their best reasons for 1 station to the class</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Exit ticket: "One place I like because ___. One place I don\'t like because ___."</p>

Reflection

Fawwaz Zahirul and Areej needed prompting to move beyond 1 reason. Comparative phrases need reinforcement Wednesday.

Wednesday DLP — Reading: City vs Village Life

R2E • 1:20–2:20 PM • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1, 3.1.2, 3.1.5 
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Set Induction</p>
<p>1. Show 2 contrasting pictures: a busy city street vs a quiet village scene<br>2. K-W-L chart: "What do you KNOW about city life? Village life?" — fill first 2 columns as a class<br>3. Preview the reading: "Today we\'ll read about someone who moved from a village to a city."</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Reading for Main Ideas, Details &amp; Writer\'s Opinion</strong></p>
<p>1. Distribute reading text: "City Life or Village Life?" (4 paragraphs, ~180 words)<br>2. Teach: main idea = what the text is about | detail = specific facts | opinion = what the writer thinks/feels<br>3. Read paragraph 1 together — identify: Main idea, 1 detail, and how the writer feels about village life<br>4. Highlight opinion words: "I prefer", "better than", "I miss", "although"</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1 — Annotation + Discussion</strong></p>
<p>1. Students read paragraphs 2-4 in pairs, annotating with 3 colours: yellow = main ideas, green = details, pink = opinions<br>2. Complete 10 comprehension questions (4 main-idea, 4 detail, 2 writer\'s opinion)<br>3. Pair discussion: "Do you agree with the writer? Which life do you prefer?"<br>4. 3 pairs share their opinion with the class</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Post-lesson</p>
<p>1. Complete the K-W-L chart: "What did you LEARN?"<br>2. One-sentence opinion: "I would prefer to live in the [city/village] because ___"</p>

Reflection

Annotation with colours was very effective — Ahmad Amsyar and Aqilah Hasnaa' correctly identified all 3 elements across the text. Identifying the writer\'s opinion was harder for some; Ng Min Hyn and Muzamin Irfan confused 'detail' with 'opinion'. The debate-style discussion was lively. 📊 Marks (Page 10): avg 12.6/14 (90%).

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align:center;font-weight:700;text-decoration:underline">Lesson Development</p>
<p><strong>Explanation 1 — Writing an Opinion Paragraph</strong></p>
<p>1. Review Wednesday\'s text — extract the writer\'s opinion structure: Statement → Reason 1 → Reason 2 → Conclusion<br>2. Model an opinion paragraph on board: "In my opinion, city life is better than village life. First, there are more job opportunities. Second, you can meet people from different cultures. <p>1. Students write a 6-8 sentence opinion paragraph comparing 2 places (8 min)<br>2. In groups of 4, each student presents their opinion (1 min each)<br>3.
