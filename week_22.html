Monday DLP — Listening Focus

I1D • 2026-06-29 • CS 1.1 / LS 1.1.2 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher shows flashcards of hobbies (reading, cycling, drawing, cooking) and says each word aloud.<br>2. Students repeat each hobby word after teacher, pointing to the correct picture.<br>3. Teacher asks "What is your hobby?" and students answer using the frame "My hobby is ______."</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Listening for Details</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher explains we will listen to short sentences about hobbies and find specific details like 'what' or 'when'.<br>2. Teacher writes on board: "I like cycling every Saturday." and asks "What hobby? (cycling) When? (Saturday)".<br>3. Teacher repeats with another example: "She draws after school." and elicits answers from students.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Listen and Fill the Table</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher gives each student a worksheet with a two-column table: Hobby and Time.<br>2. Teacher plays an audio recording of three simple sentences: (a) "I play football on Sunday." (b) "He reads books at night." (c) "They cook every evening."<br>3. Students listen and write the hobby and time in the table. Teacher pauses after each sentence and checks with the class.<br>4. Teacher asks individual students to share one answer, e.g., Muhammad Afiq Ziyad, "What is the hobby in sentence (a)?" (football).</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher reviews answers on the board and students correct their worksheets.<br>2. Students pair up and tell their partner one hobby and a time using the frame "I ______ on ______."<br>3. Teacher wraps up by asking a few students to share what they learned about their partner's hobby.</p>

Reflection

Today, Dharshayani and Mohamad Ryyan actively participated in the fill-in-the-table activity. Muhammad Thaqif Danial needed extra guidance in identifying the time details, but he improved after repetition. Overall, students showed understanding of listening for specific details about hobbies and free time.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-06-30 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.2 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show 4 pictures: reading books, playing football, drawing, cycling.<br>2. Ask students to name the hobby in each picture and point to the one they like.<br>3. Say: 'Today we read a short story about hobbies. You will find out what hobbies the children like.'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Explanation: Reading a Short Text</strong></p>
<p>1. Display the short text on the board: 'Adam likes to read books. Maya likes to draw. Sam likes to play football. They are happy.'<br>2. Read the text aloud slowly, pointing to each word.<br>3. Ask: 'What is Adam's hobby?' (Books) 'What is Maya's hobby?' (Draw) 'What is Sam's hobby?' (Football). Write answers on board.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1: Guided Reading Questions</strong></p>
<p>1. Give each pair a worksheet with the same text and 3 questions: '1. Who likes to read? 2. What does Maya like? 3. What game does Sam like?'<br>2. Read the text together as a class. Then students underline the answers in the text.<br>3. Students write the answers using sentence frames: '______ likes to read.' 'Maya likes to ______.' 'Sam likes to play ______.'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 2: Personalise Hobbies</strong></p>
<p>1. Draw a simple table on the board: Name | Hobby. Write teacher's hobby as example.<br>2. Ask each student to say their hobby. Teacher writes their name and hobby on the board.<br>3. Students copy the table into their books and write 1 sentence about their own hobby: 'My hobby is ______.'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Ask 3 students to read their sentence about their hobby.<br>2. Praise effort and display worksheets on the class board.<br>3. Remind students: 'You can read about hobbies and tell others about your hobby.'</p>

Reflection

Muhammad Syahir Haikal and Muhammad Nazrin were able to underline the correct answers from the text. Mohamad Wafy Hau needed help with the sentence frames but completed the task with guidance.

Friday DLP — Writing Focus

J1D • 2026-07-03 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.3 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show pictures of weekend hobbies (reading, cycling, playing football) and ask students to name them.<br>2. Write on board: 'My favourite weekend hobby is _______.' and choral read it.<br>3. Elicit one or two student answers using the sentence frame.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Explanation: Paragraph Structure</strong></p>
<p>1. Explain that a paragraph has 3 parts: opening sentence, details, closing sentence.<br>2. Write a model paragraph on the board: 'My favourite weekend hobby is cycling. I ride my bike at the park. I feel happy and healthy.'<br>3. Point out each part: opening (hobby), detail (where), closing (feeling).</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 1: Guided Writing</strong></p>
<p>1. Give students a worksheet with sentence starters: 'My favourite weekend hobby is ___. I ___ at ___. I feel ___.'<br>2. Help students fill in blanks using word bank: cycling, reading, playing football; park, home, field; happy, excited, tired.<br>3. Provide individual support to weaker students like Dharshayani and Ryyan to complete their sentences.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Activity 2: Peer Sharing</strong></p>
<p>1. Pair up students and ask them to read their sentences to each other.<br>2. Have pairs share one sentence with the class (e.g., Muhammad Afiq Ziyad reads his).<br>3. Encourage positive feedback: clap after each reading.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Ask students to copy their paragraph neatly into their exercise books.<br>2. Collect worksheets to check for common errors.<br>3. Summarize: 'Today we learned to write about our weekend hobby. Good job everyone!'</p>

Reflection

Students were able to use the sentence frames with support. Dharshayani needed extra help with spelling, but completed her paragraph. Mohamad Ryyan shared his hobby confidently. Muhammad Izzudin and others enjoyed sharing in pairs. More practice needed for independent writing.

Monday DLP — Listening Focus

I1E • 2026-06-29 • CS 1.1 / LS 1.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show a collage of hobby images (e.g., playing guitar, reading, cycling) and ask students to name each hobby in English.<br>2. Ask 'What is your favourite hobby?' and elicit brief responses from 2-3 students (e.g., Adzin Danish, Celine Yan-Xin).<br>3. Tell students they will listen to short descriptions of hobbies and find the main idea.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Explaining Main Ideas in Listening</strong></p>
<p>1. Explain that the main idea is the overall topic or what the speaker is talking about.<br>2. Give an example: 'If someone says I enjoy painting landscapes on weekends, the main idea is painting.'<br>3. Emphasize that they do not need every detail, just the key hobby.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Listening Activity: Hobby Descriptions</strong></p>
<p>1. Play an audio recording of three short descriptions (each 15-20 seconds) about different hobbies (e.g., photography, baking, hiking).<br>2. After each description, ask students to write down the main hobby in one word on their whiteboard.<br>3. Pair students (e.g., Adzin with Ainnur, Jayamitthra with Dhiviniesh) to compare answers and discuss any differences.<br>4. Play the audio again and then reveal the correct answers, clarifying any confusion.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Extension: Identifying Details</strong></p>
<p>1. Play a longer description (30 seconds) of a hobby with extra details (e.g., time, place, materials).<br>2. Ask students to identify not only the main hobby but also one specific detail (e.g., when they do it).<br>3. Students share their answers in groups of four, then whole-class discussion led by teacher.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Recap: ask 'What is the main idea in listening to a description?' and elicit responses from students like Keerthikan or Huzaifah.<br>2. Distribute a worksheet with three short written descriptions; students match each to a hobby picture.<br>3. Assign homework: listen to a friend or family member describe a hobby and write one sentence about the main idea.</p>

Reflection

Students performed well on identifying main hobbies; Deni and Chai showed good detail recognition. For stronger learners like Kalvin and Celine, next lesson can include inference of feelings or opinions about the hobby.

Wednesday DLP — Reading Focus

R1E • 2026-07-01 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.2 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Greet students and ask: 'What do you usually do in your free time on weekends?'<br>2. Elicit responses from 2-3 students (e.g., Adzin, Ainnur) and write keywords on the board.<br>3. Tell students they will read a short text about how two teenagers spend their free time, and answer questions to find details.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary Preview</strong></p>
<p>1. Display four vocabulary words from the text: 'relaxing', 'adventurous', 'creative', 'skill'. Define each with a simple synonym or example: relaxing = calm, adventurous = daring, creative = imaginative, skill = ability.<br>2. Read each word aloud and ask students to repeat. Then ask: 'Which word matches a person who likes hiking? (adventurous) Which word matches someone who paints? (creative)'<br>3. Instruct students to underline these words when they see them in the text.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Reading and Comprehension Questions</strong></p>
<p>1. Distribute a short text titled 'Two Teens, Two Hobbies' (about 150 words) describing Azlan, who enjoys painting (creative), and Mei, who enjoys climbing (adventurous). Include details like time spent, why they like it, and a skill they learned.<br>2. Ask students to read the text silently and answer 5 detail questions on a worksheet (e.g., What is Azlan's hobby? How often does Mei climb? What skill did Azlan learn?). Allow 10 minutes.<br>3. After reading, have students pair up with a partner (e.g., Celine & Chai, Deni & Dhiviniesh) to compare answers and discuss any differences.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Answer Check and Discussion</strong></p>
<p>1. Go through the answers as a class, calling on different students (e.g., Huzaifah for Q1, Jayamitthra for Q2) to share their answer and explain how they found it in the text.<br>2. Highlight any questions that require inferring (e.g., 'Why does Mei say climbing teaches her patience?') and discuss the clue in the text.<br>3. Clarify any vocabulary or sentence structures that were challenging.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Personal Connection Writing</strong></p>
<p>1. Instruct students to write 3-4 sentences about their own free time activity, using at least one of the vocabulary words (relaxing, adventurous, creative, skill). Example: 'I like reading because it is relaxing. It also improves my vocabulary skill.'.<br>2. Ask volunteers (e.g., Kalvin, Keerthikan) to read their sentences aloud to the class.<br>3. Collect the writing for informal assessment of understanding.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Distribute a sticky note to each student and ask them to write one new thing they learned about a classmate's hobby from today's lesson.<br>2. Collect the sticky notes and quickly share 2-3 examples (anonymously) to reinforce the theme.<br>3. Assign optional homework: Find a short article in English about an unusual hobby and be ready to share one fact next lesson.</p>

Reflection

Today's lesson went well with strong class engagement. Adzin Danish and Ainnur Raudhah provided insightful answers during the vocabulary preview and reading discussion, demonstrating good understanding of detail questions. Celine and Chai showed effective pair work, and Keerthikan's personal writing used vocabulary appropriately. Next steps: Encourage more inferential questions for deeper comprehension.

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1E • 2026-07-02 • CS 4.2 / LS 4.2.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show a picture of a person playing the guitar with a speech bubble saying 'I love playing guitar because it makes me feel calm.'<br>2. Ask students: 'What is the person's hobby? Why do they like it?'<br>3. Elicit a few responses and write keywords on the board: hobby, reason, feeling.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Structure of a Paragraph about a Hobby</strong></p>
<p>1. State the hobby in the first sentence.<br>2. Explain why you like it with a reason.<br>3. Describe how you do it or when you do it.<br>4. Conclude with how it makes you feel.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Guided Writing: Hobby Paragraph</strong></p>
<p>1. Provide a sentence scaffold: 'My favourite hobby is ______. I like it because ______. I usually do it ______. It makes me feel ______.'<br>2. Ask students to fill in the blanks individually for 3 minutes.<br>3. Pair share: Celine Yan-Xin Phee and Chai Zi Jian share their sentences with each other and give one suggestion for improvement.<br>4. Collect a few examples on the board; correct punctuation (capital letters, full stops) as a class.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Using Connectors and Details</strong></p>
<p>1. Add details using 'such as', 'for example', 'especially'.<br>2. Show a model paragraph: 'My favourite hobby is reading. I like it because it helps me relax. I usually read before bed, especially fantasy books. It makes me feel adventurous.'<br>3. Highlight the connector 'especially' and the detail 'fantasy books'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Independent Paragraph Writing</strong></p>
<p>1. Instruct students to write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences) about their own favourite hobby.<br>2. Remind them to include: hobby, reason, when/how, feeling, and at least one connector or detail.<br>3. Dhiviniesh Rao and Keerthikan write on the whiteboard while others write in their books.<br>4. After 7 minutes, peers check each other's punctuation (Adzin Danish checks Ainnur Raudhah's; Deni Aago checks Huzaifah's).</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Select two students (Jayamitthra and Kalvin Pillai) to read their paragraphs aloud.<br>2. Ask the class to identify one strength and one area for improvement for each.<br>3. Summarise key points: state hobby, give reason, use correct punctuation and details.</p>

Reflection

Students demonstrated good understanding of paragraph structure; Celine Yan-Xin Phee and Chai Zi Jian provided detailed sentences with connectors. A few students (e.g., Deni Aago) needed reminders on full stops, but peer checking helped. Overall, the class worked well independently and showed critical thinking in adding details.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>

<p><strong>Paragraph Structure</strong></p>
<p>1. Show an example on the board: 'My favourite hobby is reading fantasy novels because it lets me escape into magical worlds. I usually read after school for an hour. The best part is imagining the characters and places. Reading is the perfect way to end my day.'<br>3. Highlight the topic sentence (first), details (second and third), and conclusion (last). Use the structure: topic sentence, 2-3 supporting details, concluding sentence.<br>2. After 10 minutes, exchange papers with another pair. Read the paragraph and check: Is there a clear topic sentence? Are there at least two details? Does it end well? Write one positive comment and one suggestion.<br>3. Return papers and allow 3 minutes to revise based on feedback. Volunteers share their final version (e.g., AFIF, AAYRA).</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Collect all paragraphs for teacher assessment, focusing on structure and creativity.<br>2. Assign homework: Write a new paragraph about a different hobby, using the same structure, and bring it next class.</p>

Reflection

Today’s writing activity showed strong understanding of paragraph structure, especially from AAYRA and CHOYE. Some students like DHIVENASSH needed extra support with linking ideas, but pair work helped them. The peer feedback was effective and saved time for individual attention.

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1M • 2026-07-02 • CS 4.2 / LS 4.2.3 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Display an image of a hobby (e.g., reading) on screen without captions.<br>2. Ask: "What activity is shown?" and "What do you like to do in your free time?" to elicit responses.<br>3. State objective: Today, you will plan, draft, and revise a two-paragraph description of your favourite free time activity.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Structure of a Two-Paragraph Description</strong></p>
<p>1. Explain that paragraph 1 should introduce the hobby and why you started it.<br>2. Explain that paragraph 2 should describe how you do it, when you do it, and why you enjoy it.<br>3. Provide a sample paragraph about reading: 'I love reading novels because it helps me relax after school. I first started reading when my mother gave me a mystery book...' and discuss key elements.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Planning Your Description</strong></p>
<p>1. Distribute a planning template with four boxes: 'Hobby', 'Why I started', 'How I do it', 'Why I enjoy it'.<br>2. Instruct students to jot down key words or phrases for each box individually.<br>3. After 5 minutes, have students share their ideas with a partner and refine their plan.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Drafting with Transition Words</strong></p>
<p>1. Show a list of transition words: 'First', 'Next', 'Also', 'Moreover', 'Finally'.<br>2. Model writing the first paragraph of a sample description, using 'First' and 'Also'.<br>3. Remind students to use these words to connect ideas within and between paragraphs.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Drafting and Revising</strong></p>
<p>1. Ask students to write their two-paragraph description based on their plan (15 minutes).<br>2. After writing, pair students to exchange drafts and provide two positive comments and one suggestion for improvement.<br>3. Allow 5 minutes for students to revise their own work based on feedback.</p>
