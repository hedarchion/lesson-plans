## 1 Endurance (1E)
**Skill:** Listening | **CS:** 1.1 | **LS:** 1.1.1

### Lesson Plan
```html

Set Induction


1. Greet the class. Quick warm-up: “What did you do last weekend?” Take 2-3 volunteers (e.g., Celine, Ainnur Raudhah, Huzaifah). Connect their answers to hobbies.
2. Show 4 hobby images on screen (cycling, baking, gaming, drawing) without labels. Ask: “What hobbies do you see? What words do you associate with each?”
3. Elicit hobby-related verbs and adjectives: ride, bake, sketch, level up, relaxing, challenging, competitive. Write them on the board.


 


Lesson Development


Explanation 1


1. State the LO: “Today you will listen to short descriptions of hobbies and identify the main ideas.”
2. Strategy tip: “Listen for the hobby name first, then listen for WHY the person enjoys it. The main idea is usually hobby + reason.”
3. Pre-teach key vocabulary: relaxing, challenging, rewarding, creative, competitive. Quick choral drilling.


 


Activity 1 — Guided Practice (whole class)


1. “Listen to Track 1. Write down the hobby and one main idea.” Play audio (teacher reads aloud):

“I love spending time in the kitchen on weekends. I try new recipes from different countries. The best part is when my family tastes what I made and smiles. It makes me feel creative and happy.”

2. Elicit answers: Hobby = cooking/baking. Main idea = trying new recipes, making family happy, feels creative.
3. Write on board: Track 1 -> Baking/Cooking + Creativity & Family. This is a model of what main ideas look like.
4. Play Track 2:

“Every Saturday morning, I grab my racket and head to the court. I play with my friends for about two hours. We run around, hit the shuttlecock, and laugh a lot. It keeps me fit and I love the competition.”

5. Pair-share (30 sec): Adzin Danish & Kalvin, Nurin Mirza & Krettisha — compare answers before class discussion.
6. Class check: Hobby = badminton. Main ideas = fitness, friendship, competition.


 


Activity 2 — Independent Practice (pairs)


1. “Two more tracks. Work with your partner. For each, write one sentence: ‘The hobby is ___ and the main idea is ___.’”
2. Play Track 3:

“After school, I sit at my desk and open my sketchbook. I draw whatever comes to mind — sometimes people, sometimes nature. Hours pass and I do not even notice. It is my way of relaxing after a long day.”

3. Play Track 4:

“I spend about an hour every evening in my room, pressing buttons on my controller. I play adventure games where I explore new worlds and solve puzzles. Some people say it is a waste of time, but for me, it is like reading a book that I can control.”

4. Give pairs 2 minutes to write their answers. Walk around and check. Note strong pairs (e.g., Jayamitthra & Sharveni) who may share first.
5. Call 3-4 pairs to share. Expected answers: Track 3 = Drawing + Relaxation/Escape; Track 4 = Gaming + Adventure/Problem-solving.
6. Class vote: thumbs up/down on each answer. Clarify any misunderstandings.


 


Post-lesson


1. Quick recap: “What strategy did we use today?” — Listen for the hobby name, then listen for the reason.
2. Exit ticket (on a slip of paper): “Write ONE hobby you enjoy and ONE reason why.”
3. Collect tickets as students leave. Use responses to gauge understanding and plan next lesson.
4. Reminder: Smart Task 2 is still pending for 14 students. Submission closes this week.

```

### Reflection
The listening tracks worked well for this mid-strong group — most students correctly identified hobby + main idea from the short descriptions. Celine and Jayamitthra were quick to volunteer answers, while Deni Aago and Nur Qisya Anggun needed slight prompting during the pair-share phase. The exit tickets will give a clearer picture of individual comprehension. On the admin side, Smart Task 2 submission remains a concern: only 12 of 26 students have submitted. A reminder was included in the post-lesson to push for completion by end of week.

# SELASA (Tuesday)

## 1 Devotion (1D)
**Skill:** Reading | **CS:** 3.1 | **LS:** 3.1.2

### Lesson Plan
```html

Set Induction


1. Greet the class. Display four simple pictures on the board: a bicycle, a comic book, a pan with food, and a pencil drawing. Ask: "What can you see? What do these things have in common?" -- guide students to say "hobbies" or "free time."
2. Ask 2-3 students: "Ryyan, what is your hobby?" "Dharshayani, do you like drawing?" Keep it quick. Accept short answers.
3. Say: "Today we will read about our friends Ryyan, Firdaus, Wardah, and Dharshayani. We will find out what they like to do in their free time."


 


Lesson Development


Explanation 1 -- Pre-reading (vocabulary)


1. Write key words on the board: hobby, free time, collect, enjoy, every weekend, sketchbook, chef.
2. Say each word clearly. Students repeat chorally.
3. Give a simple definition for each. Use gestures or pictures. Example: "A sketchbook is a book you use to draw."
4. Check understanding: "Thaqif Umar, what is a hobby?" "Nur Amni, what does 'enjoy' mean?"


 


Activity 1 -- Reading the passage


1. Display the passage on the screen or give a worksheet:

My Friends' Hobbies

Hello! My name is Dharshayani. I have three friends: Ryyan, Firdaus, and Wardah. We all have different hobbies.

Ryyan's hobby is cycling. He rides his bicycle every Saturday morning. He cycles at the park near his house. He feels happy when he cycles.

Firdaus's hobby is reading comics. He reads at home every night before bed. He has ten comic books. His favourite comic is about a superhero.

Wardah's hobby is cooking. She cooks with her mother every Sunday. She can make nasi goreng and kuih. She wants to be a chef one day.

I like drawing. I draw after school. I have a sketchbook. I draw my family and friends. Drawing makes me calm.

2. Teacher reads the passage aloud. Students follow with their fingers.
3. Read the passage again together as a class (choral reading).
4. Ask: "What is Ryyan's hobby?" (cycling), "What is Wardah's hobby?" (cooking). Quick choral answers.


 


Explanation 2 -- Comprehension strategy


1. Draw a simple table on the board with four rows (Name | Hobby | When | Where).
2. Fill in Row 1 together: Ryyan | cycling | Saturday morning | at the park.
3. Point out: "The answers are in the text. We look for key words like 'hobby is', 'every', 'at the'."
4. Say: "Nazrin, what is the key word to find Ryyan's hobby?" Guide: hobby is cycling.


 


Activity 2 -- Table completion (pair work)


1. Give each pair (e.g., Izzudin with Harith, Nik Mikhael with Thaqif Danial, Santhiya with Thanantigally) a worksheet with the table:

| Name | Hobby | When | Where |
|------|-------|------|-------|
| Ryyan | cycling | Saturday morning | at the park |
| Firdaus | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Wardah | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| Dharshayani | ____ | ____ | ____ |

2. Students read the passage and fill in the table with their partner.
3. Teacher walks around. Help struggling students -- point to the right paragraph. Ask Thaqif Umar and Riszuwán: "Where does Firdaus read? Look at the third sentence."
4. Check answers as a class. Complete the table on the board.
5. Praise pairs who finish: "Well done, Nur Dayana and Nur Faqihah!"


 


Activity 3 -- Comprehension questions


1. Students answer these questions in their exercise book. Provide sentence frames on the board.

Q1: What is Firdaus's hobby?
Frame: Firdaus's hobby is __________.

Q2: When does Wardah cook?
Frame: Wardah cooks __________.

Q3: Where does Ryyan cycle?
Frame: Ryyan cycles __________.

Q4: How does Dharshayani feel when she draws?
Frame: Dharshayani feels __________ when she draws.

2. Weaker students copy the frame and fill in one word. Stronger students write full sentences.
3. Pair check -- Nur'Irish checks with Thishanthan.
4. Teacher writes answers on the board. Students self-correct.


 


Post-lesson


1. Quick recap: "Muhamad Wafy, what is Firdaus's hobby?" "Muslimahtul, what is Wardah's hobby?"
2. Exit ticket (oral or written on a slip): "One hobby from the story I like is __________." Students share with the class -- ask 3-4 volunteers (Afiq Ziyad, Indrianna, Syahir Haikal, Tharinish).
3. Praise the class: "Good reading today, 1 Devotion! You found details from the text. Next time we will write about our own hobbies."

```

### Reflection
The reading passage with real student names (Dharshayani, Ryyan, Firdaus, Wardah) was engaging -- students paid more attention when they saw their friends in the text. The picture-table comprehension strategy worked well for the A2 Revise group; most pairs could extract at least 3 out of 4 rows correctly. Thaqif Umar and Riszuwán needed individual support to find 'where' information -- next time, colour-code the paragraphs to make scanning easier. The sentence frames for Activity 3 helped weaker students produce full answers without frustration. Formative M19-M Writing average is 4.2/8 (53%) -- this reading lesson builds foundational comprehension skills that will support their next writing task on hobbies.

### Lesson Plan
```html

Set Induction
```

```html
my hobby is baking i bake cakes and cookies every weekend i love decorating them with frosting and sprinkles my family says they are delicious do you like baking too

Lesson Development


Explanation 1 — Punctuation Rules Review


1. State the LO: “By the end of this lesson, you will write a short paragraph about your favourite hobby using capitals, full stops, commas in lists, question marks, and speech marks correctly.”
2. Draw a Punctuation Quick Reference on the board:

• Capital letter -> start of a sentence, names, ‘I’
• Full stop (.) -> end of a sentence
• Comma (,) -> separates items in a list; after ‘First,’ ‘Also,’ etc.
• Question mark (?) -> ends a question
• Speech marks (“ ”) -> around words someone says aloud

3. Give one example per rule on the board and say each aloud. Students repeat chorally. Example: “I like reading, drawing, and cycling.” — point out the comma in the list.


 


Activity 1 — Fix the Paragraph (pair work)

My hobby is baking. I bake cakes and cookies every weekend. I love decorating them with frosting and sprinkles. My family says, “They are delicious!” Do you like baking too?

5. Discuss: Where did we use commas? (in the list “cakes and cookies”). Where did we use speech marks? (around “They are delicious!”). Where did we use a question mark? (at the end of the question).

2. Ask students to identify each punctuation type in the model. Circle them together as a class.
• Capitals: My, I, First, Then, What
• Full stops at end of each sentence
• Commas: “healthy, happy, and relaxed”; “First,”; “Then,”
• Question marks: “Can I come with you?”, “What is your favourite hobby?”
• Speech marks: around the dialogue parts
3. Emphasise: “A well-punctuated paragraph is clear, professional, and easy to read. Readers trust your writing when punctuation is correct.”
```
