Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-06-29 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show a picture of a school building with different rooms labelled in the local language.<br>2. Ask 'What places do you see in school?' and invite students to point and say in Malay.<br>3. Introduce the English words for three places: canteen, library, hall, saying each slowly and having students repeat chorally.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary Introduction</strong></p>
<p>1. Display flashcards of canteen, library, and hall one by one, each with a clear image and the word.<br>2. Say each word clearly, emphasizing the sounds (e.g., can-TEEN, LI-bra-ry, hall), and have the class repeat after you.<br>3. For each word, ask a simple question like 'Do you eat in the library?' to check understanding, and correct with a nod or headshake.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Guided Pair Speaking - 'I go to the...'</strong></p>
<p>1. Model the sentence frame: 'I go to the [place] to [activity].' For example, 'I go to the canteen to eat.'<br>2. Write on the board: 'I go to the _____ to _____ .' and provide a word bank: canteen/eat, library/read, hall/play.<br>3. In pairs (e.g., DHARSHAYANI with MOHAMAD RYYAN), students take turns completing the sentence orally. The teacher circulates and helps weaker pairs.<br>4. After 5 minutes, ask a few pairs to share their sentences with the class, praising correct pronunciation and effort.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Matching Game - Place & Action</strong></p>
<p>1. Prepare three large cards with place names and three with activities (eat, read, play).<br>2. Call up two students (e.g., MOHAMAD WAFY HAU and MUHAMAD HARITH AQASHA) to match a place with an activity, then say the full sentence: 'I go to the [place] to [activity].'<br>3. Repeat with different pairs until all students have had a turn, providing support as needed.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Quick recap: Point to each flashcard and ask 'What is this?' - students shout the word.<br>2. Distribute a worksheet with three pictures (canteen, library, hall) and blanks for a sentence. Students choose one place and write 'I go to the ______ to ______.' using the word bank.<br>3. Collect worksheets as exit tickets to assess understanding.</p>

Reflection

Most students could repeat the vocabulary with support, but DHARSHAYANI and MOHAMAD RYYAN struggled with pronunciation of 'library' - they will need extra drilling next lesson. MUHAMMAD NAZRIN and MUHAMMAD THAQIF DANIAL confidently produced full sentences during pair work, showing good progress.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-06-30 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show flashcards of school places (library, field, classroom, canteen) and ask "What place is this?" for choral repetition.<br>2. Say "I see a library. I see a field. What do you see?" and have students point to the correct flashcard.<br>3. Sing a simple song: "We go to school, we read in the library, we play on the field, we eat in the canteen" with actions.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Reading Aloud</strong></p>
<p>1. Write on board a short passage: "This is my school. I go to the library. I read books. I go to the field. I play football. I go to the canteen. I eat noodles."<br>2. Read the passage aloud slowly, pointing to each word, and have students repeat each sentence chorally.<br>3. Highlight key words: library, field, canteen, and ask students to say them again.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Comprehension Questions</strong></p>
<p>1. Give each student a worksheet with three questions: "Where do I read books?" "Where do I play football?" "Where do I eat noodles?" with sentence frames like "I read books in the _____".<br>2. Read each question aloud, then guide students to find the answer in the passage and fill the blanks using choral repetition.<br>3. Call students individually (e.g., Dharshanayani, Mohamad Ryyan) to read their completed sentences for the class.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary Reinforcement</strong></p>
<p>1. On the board, list three places: library, field, canteen. Draw simple icons (book, ball, food) next to each.<br>2. Say sentences like "I borrow books from the ____" and have students guess the place, then say full sentence chorally.<br>3. Repeat with "I play on the ____" and "I eat in the ____".</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Matching Game</strong></p>
<p>1. Prepare a worksheet with pictures of library, field, canteen on the left and sentences on the right (e.g., "I read here." "I play here." "I eat here.").<br>2. Read each sentence aloud, and have students draw a line from the sentence to the correct picture.<br>3. Check answers together by calling out sentences and having students point to the picture.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Recap: "What did we learn today? We read about places in school. What are the places?" (library, field, canteen).<br>2. Exit ticket: Give each student a small paper and ask them to draw one place from the passage and write its name with help if needed.</p>

Reflection

Today, Muhamad Nazrin confidently read the passage aloud and answered questions correctly. Mohamad Wafy Hau struggled with the word 'canteen' but improved after choral repetition. Next time, I will add more picture support for weaker students like Muhammad Syahir Haikal.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>

Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1E • 2026-06-29 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show a picture of a clock with activities around it (e.g., wake up, brush teeth) and ask 'What do you do every day?' to elicit prior knowledge.<br>2. Write student responses on the board, highlighting time expressions like 'at 7 o'clock' and 'in the morning'.<br>3. Play a short video clip of a person's daily routine (no audio) and ask students to guess the verbs and times shown.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary and Time Expressions</strong></p>
<p>1. Introduce key daily routine verbs: wake up, get up, have breakfast, go to school, have lunch, do homework, have dinner, watch TV, go to bed.<br>2. Present time expressions: at + specific time (e.g., at 7:00), in the morning/afternoon/evening, at night, before/after + activity.<br>3. Model sentences using the vocabulary and ask students to repeat: 'I wake up at 6:30 in the morning.'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pair Interview: My Daily Routine</strong></p>
<p>1. In pairs (students A and B), each student writes down their own daily routine using the target vocabulary and time expressions.<br>2. Student A interviews Student B using questions like 'What time do you wake up?' and 'What do you do after school?'<br>3. Student B answers in full sentences, and Student A takes notes. Then they switch roles.<br>4. After both interviews, each student shares one interesting fact about their partner's routine with the class.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Group Guessing Game</strong></p>
<p>1. Divide the class into groups of 3-4 (e.g., group includes Chai Zi Jian, Huzaifah, and Celine).<br>2. Each group receives a set of cards with daily routine verbs (e.g., 'have dinner', 'go to bed') and time expression cards (e.g., 'at 8:00 pm', 'in the evening').<br>3. One student picks a verb card and a time card, then acts it out silently while others guess the sentence: 'You are having dinner at 8:00 pm.'<br>4. Rotate roles until all members have acted and guessed. The group with the most correct sentences wins.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Ask three students to volunteer and share their partner's routine in front of the class, using connectors like 'and then', 'after that'.<br>2. Conduct a quick error correction on common mistakes (e.g., 'I go to bed at 10 o'clock' vs 'I go to bed 10 o'clock').</p>

Reflection

Today's speaking activities engaged stronger students like Deni and Jayamitthra deeper in pair interviews, while Keerthikan and Dhiviniesh improved their use of time expressions. The group guessing game fostered teamwork and confidence in speaking. Overall, the class demonstrated good fluency, but some students like Huzaifah need more practice with 'at' vs 'in' for time.

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1E • 2026-07-02 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.5 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show a short video (30 seconds) of a student's daily routine without audio, and ask students to guess the activities.<br>2. Elicit daily routine verbs (wake up, eat breakfast, go to school) and write them on the board as a word bank.<br>3. Say: 'Now we will write about a friend's routine. First, let's see an example.'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Model Paragraph</strong></p>
<p>1. Display a model paragraph on the board: 'My friend Adzin wakes up at 6 a.m. First, he brushes his teeth. Then, he eats breakfast. After that, he goes to school. Finally, he does his homework in the evening.'<br>2. Underline connectors: 'First', 'Then', 'After that', 'Finally'. Explain they link steps in order.<br>3. Ask students to identify the subject pronouns (he) and possessive adjectives (his) used for one person.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pair Interview and Write</strong></p>
<p>1. Pair up students: e.g., Ainnur with Celine, Dhiviniesh with Jayamitthra, etc. Each student interviews their partner about their daily routine using questions like 'What time do you wake up?' and 'What do you do after school?'<br>2. Students take notes on a provided worksheet with guiding questions (wake up, breakfast, school, after school, evening).<br>3. Individually, each student writes a short paragraph (5-6 sentences) about their partner's routine using the model and connectors. Encourage adding one extra detail (e.g., 'He likes to read before bed.').<br>4. Teacher circulates to support weaker students (e.g., Deni, Huzaifah) with sentence starters.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Choose two volunteers (e.g., Kalvin and Keerthikan) to read their paragraphs aloud to the class.<br>2. Peer feedback: Partners swap paragraphs and check for correct use of connectors and pronouns using a simple checklist.<br>3. Collect paragraphs for assessment.</p>

Reflection

Most students successfully wrote coherent paragraphs with connectors. Adzin and Celine demonstrated strong use of time order words, while Jayamitthra needed minimal support with pronouns. A few students like Deni and Huzaifah required scaffolding but improved with sentence starters.

Tuesday DLP — Speaking Focus

S1M • 2026-06-30 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Show a slide with 10 hobby images (e.g., reading, cycling, gaming) and ask students to name them in English as a quick recall.<br>2. Elicit one or two less common hobbies (e.g., origami, calligraphy) to extend vocabulary.<br>3. Ask: 'How many of you play video games?' and do a quick show of hands to model a survey question.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>

Model key survey phrases: 'What is your favourite hobby?', 'Which hobbies do you do in your free time?', 'How often do you...?'<br>3. Introduce the tally chart template: columns for hobby name, tally marks, and total.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Class Hobby Survey & Presentation</strong></p>
<p>1. In pairs (A and B), students take turns asking each other about their hobbies using the modelled questions and record answers with tally marks on the handout.<br>2. After 5 minutes, combine pairs into groups of four. Each student shares their partner's responses, and the group compiles a complete tally for their four members.<br>3. Select three groups (e.g., Auni & Gan, Fatim & Hadwin, Afif & Dhivenassh) to present their tally chart to the class, speaking in full sentences: 'In our group, the most popular hobby is... because...'<br>4. As a class, compile the data into a master tally on the board, and discuss the overall favourite hobby in 1 Meekness.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students write two sentences in their journal: 'In my class, the most common hobby is ___ because ___.' and 'One new hobby I learned today is ___.'<br>2. Quick pair share: each student reads their sentences to a partner and receives one piece of feedback.</p>

Reflection

Most students confidently used the target vocabulary during pair work, especially Auni and Gan who produced extended responses. Afif, however, needed minimal prompting to switch from Malay to English. The survey activity successfully encouraged critical thinking as Dhivenassh asked follow-up questions like 'Why do you like it?'.
