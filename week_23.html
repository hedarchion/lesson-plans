Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-07-06 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher plays a recording of different animal sounds (cat, dog, bird, cow) and asks the class to guess what animals they are.<br>2. Teacher shows large colorful picture cards of the animals and models the pronunciation clearly.<br>3. Students repeat the animal names chorally and mimic the animal sounds to build excitement and interest.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary and Sentence Frame Presentation</strong></p>
<p>Teacher introduces four target animals: cat, dog, cow, and duck using flashcards on the board.<br>Teacher introduces a simple, highly scaffolded sentence frame: 'It is a [animal]. It says [sound].'<br>Teacher models the sentence frame: 'It is a cat. It says meow.' using expressive gestures.<br>Students practice the sentence structure chorally as a whole class three times to build familiarity.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Animal Sound Matching Game</strong></p>
<p>Teacher divides the class into pairs, pairing weaker pupils with slightly stronger peers for peer support.<br>Each pair is given a set of animal picture cards and corresponding sound word cards.<br>Students work together to match the correct animal picture with its sound card (e.g., dog with 'woof').<br>Teacher circulates to assist pupils like Mohamad Ryyan and Muhammad Afiq Ziyad who need extra guidance with matching.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pair Speaking Practice</strong></p>
<p>Using the matched cards, students take turns to point to an animal card and speak to their partner.<br>Student A asks: 'What is it?' and Student B responds using the frame: 'It is a [animal]. It says [sound].'<br>Dharshayani and Mohamad Wafy Hau practice speaking aloud, supported by the sentence frame prominently displayed on the whiteboard.<br>Teacher monitors and praises students who make an effort to use the full sentence frame.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher holds up an animal flashcard randomly and calls on selected pupils to say the animal name and its sound aloud.<br>2. Students clean up their desks and receive positive verbal feedback and stickers for their active participation.</p>

Reflection

Most students successfully achieved the learning objective by matching and naming the animals with guidance. Mohamad Ryyan and Muhammad Afiq Ziyad required close monitoring but managed to say the simple animal sounds, while Dharshayani showed excellent confidence in leading her pair during the speaking activity.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-07-07 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher shows flashcards of common pets (cat, dog, rabbit, fish) and mimics their sounds to capture attention.<br>2. Students guess the animals and repeat the names chorally to practice pronunciation.<br>3. Teacher introduces today's topic: Reading about our favorite pets using simple sentences.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary and Sentence Frames Introduction</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces key vocabulary words (fur, tail, paws, swim, hop) using pictures.<br>2. Teacher models reading a simple description of a cat: 'This is a cat. It has soft fur. It can jump.'<br>3. Students practice reading the sentence frames aloud using choral repetition.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Guided Group Reading and Matching</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides the class into small groups and hands out simple animal description cards.<br>2. Students work in groups to read the text and match it with the correct animal picture card.<br>3. Teacher guides weaker students like Mohamad Ryyan and Muhammad Nazrin by pointing to key adjectives.<br>4. Each group reads their matched description card aloud to the class.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Fill-in-the-Blanks Comprehension Worksheet</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher distributes a highly guided worksheet with a short passage about a pet rabbit.<br>2. Students use a word bank (hop, long, ears, pet) to complete the missing words in the passage.<br>3. Teacher walks around to assist and provide positive reinforcement to students during the writing phase.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students share one thing they learned about their favorite pet today with their partner.<br>2. Teacher summarizes the lesson and praises the class for their active participation.</p>

Reflection

Most students successfully achieved the learning objective by matching descriptions to the correct animals. Dharshayani A/P Paul and Muhammad Izzudin showed great improvement in reading simple sentence frames aloud. However, Mohamad Ryyan and Muhammad Nazrin still require more guided reading support in future lessons.

Friday DLP — Writing Focus

J1D • 2026-07-10 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>Teacher shows flashcards of common pets -- a cat, a dog, a rabbit, and a fish -- and mimics their sounds to grab the students' attention.<br>Teacher leads a choral repetition of the animal names, ensuring students like Mohamad Ryyan and Muhammad Firdaus participate actively.<br>Teacher asks the class to share if they have any of these pets at home using a simple verbal prompt: 'Who has a cat?'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>

It can [action].'<br>Teacher models how to fill in the blanks using a picture of a cat: 'This is a cat. It is orange. <p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>My Favourite Animal Writing Task</strong></p>
<p>Students choose their own favourite animal and write 3 to 4 simple sentences describing it in their exercise books using the scaffolded frames.<br>Students draw a simple picture of their chosen animal next to their sentences.<br>Teacher walks around the classroom to assist Muhammad Afiq Ziyad and Muhammad Izzudin with spelling and basic punctuation.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>Teacher invites a few students, including Muhammad Thaqif Danial, to stand up and read their 3-sentence descriptions aloud to the class.<br>Teacher praises all students for their effort and collects the exercise books for assessment of Writing Standard 4.1.1.</p>

Reflection

Most students successfully wrote 3 simple sentences using the provided sentence frames. Dharshayani A/P Paul and Mohamad Wafy Hau showed great progress in writing complete sentences with teacher guidance. A few students still struggle with capitalization and will receive targeted support in the next lesson.

Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1E • 2026-07-06 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher shows high-quality pictures of two contrasting Malaysian locations: Bukit Bintang, Kuala Lumpur (busy, noisy) and Mossy Forest, Cameron Highlands (quiet, beautiful).<br>2. Teacher asks students to describe what they see using only single adjectives, writing their responses on the whiteboard.<br>3. Teacher introduces the lesson objective: to describe various places using advanced adjectives and connectors.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Exploring Place Adjectives and Connectors</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces a list of place adjectives (e.g., bustling, serene, picturesque, spacious, crowded) alongside basic counterparts (busy, quiet, beautiful, big).<br>2. Teacher explains how to use connectors like 'although', 'but', and 'and' to build complex sentences -- for example, 'Although Bukit Bintang is crowded, it is very exciting.'<br>3. Teacher models correct pronunciation and intonation for the new vocabulary.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>The Travel Agent Role-Play</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are paired up. CELINE YAN-XIN PHEE and JAYAMITTHRA A/P VIKNESH WARAN are selected to demonstrate the model dialogue first.<br>2. In pairs, one student acts as a travel agent recommending a place, while the other acts as a tourist asking questions about the destination.<br>3. Students must use at least three newly learned adjectives and one connector in their spoken dialogue.<br>4. Teacher moves around the classroom to facilitate, providing constructive feedback on pronunciation and vocabulary usage.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Class Showcase and Peer Evaluation</strong></p>
<p>1. Selected pairs, such as CHAI ZI JIAN and DHIVINIESH RAO A/L LAGES RAO, present their travel dialogue to the class.<br>2. Peer evaluators are assigned to listen for correct usage of adjectives and connectors.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher conducts a quick exit-ticket activity where each student must state one adjective and one sentence describing their hometown.<br>2. Teacher summarizes the key vocabulary learned and previews the next lesson on writing descriptive paragraphs.</p>

Reflection

Most students successfully achieved the learning objective by confidently using advanced place adjectives during the role-play. CELINE YAN-XIN PHEE and JAYAMITTHRA A/P VIKNESH WARAN showed excellent command of connectors like 'although' and 'whereas' in their spoken descriptions. However, HUZAIFAH BIN MOHD ADHA MIZAN required minor prompting to use the correct adjective forms, which will be addressed in the next speaking warm-up.

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1E • 2026-07-09 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.5 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>Teacher displays a high-quality image of a vibrant Malaysian destination (e.g., Kundasang or Georgetown) on the board.<br>Teacher asks students to brainstorm and call out sensory adjectives -- what they can see, hear, smell, and feel in the picture.<br>Teacher connects the brainstormed words to the day's objective: writing a structured, descriptive paragraph of a place using rich vocabulary and connectors.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Structuring a Descriptive Paragraph with Connectors</strong></p>
<p>Teacher introduces the descriptive paragraph framework, emphasizing the hook, sensory details, and a concluding feeling.<br>Teacher introduces advanced cohesive devices such as 'furthermore', 'in contrast', and 'consequently' to elevate their writing.<br>Teacher showcases a model paragraph on the board, guiding students to identify sensory adjectives and connectors.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Independent Writing and Peer Editing</strong></p>
<p>Students choose a specific place they know well and independently write a descriptive paragraph of 80 to 100 words.<br>Students are required to include at least three sensory details and two complex connectors in their drafts.<br>Students swap paragraphs with a partner to check for descriptive impact and correct use of connectors using a peer-editing rubric.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>A few students are selected to read their completed descriptive paragraphs aloud to the class.<br>Teacher summarizes the key elements of descriptive writing and collects the final paragraphs for assessment.</p>

Reflection

The lesson was highly successful, with the majority of the students demonstrating a strong grasp of sensory descriptions. Celine Yan-Xin Phee and Jayamitthra wrote exceptionally vivid paragraphs, while Adzin Danish and Huzaifah showed great improvement in integrating complex connectors.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Introduction to Food Groups and Nutrition Vocabulary</strong></p>
<p>Teacher introduces key nutritional terms, including carbohydrates, protein, unsaturated fats, dietary fiber, vitamins, and minerals, using a mind map on the whiteboard.<br>Teacher explains how to construct complex sentences to describe food functions, using subordinating conjunctions such as 'which' and 'because' (e.g., 'Fish is an excellent source of protein, which is essential for muscle repair.').<br>Teacher models a short interactive dialogue with Auni Binti Mohamad Yusaini, demonstrating how to ask and answer questions about dietary habits using the new vocabulary.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>The Balanced Plate Role-Play</strong></p>
<p>Students are paired up (e.g., Aayra Faiz Firman with Ahmad Harraz, and Fatin Aqilah with Gan Kok Xian) and given a template of a 'Healthy Plate'.<br>Each pair must design a nutritionally balanced meal and practice a spoken dialogue where one student acts as a nutritionist and the other as a client seeking dietary advice.<br>Students must use at least four specific nutrition vocabulary words and connect their sentences using cohesive devices like 'moreover' and 'consequently'.<br>Teacher monitors the class, providing on-the-spot feedback on pronunciation and grammatical accuracy.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Nutritionist Showcase</strong></p>
<p>Selected pairs, including Hadwin and Afif Irsyaduddin, present their role-play dialogues to the rest of the class.<br>The audience listening must identify the food groups and nutrition terms used by the presenters and write them down in their notebooks.<br>Teacher facilitates a peer-evaluation session where students highlight the strengths of their classmates' spoken delivery and sentence structures.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>Teacher conducts a quick-fire 'Nutrition Trivia' session where students must verbally match a food item to its primary nutrient within five seconds.<br>Teacher summarizes the lesson by emphasizing the importance of a balanced diet and praises the class for their active participation and expressive vocabulary usage.</p>

Reflection

The lesson was highly successful as the majority of the students in 1 Meekness demonstrated a strong grasp of the nutrition vocabulary. Choye Kai En and Dhivenassh produced exceptionally mature complex sentences during their role-play, while Aayra Faiz Firman showed great improvement in using transition markers naturally. A few students will need continued reinforcement on pronouncing multi-syllabic words like 'carbohydrates' in the upcoming lessons.

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Introduction to Family Vocabulary and Sentence Frames</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher presents flashcards of family members and basic adjectives like 'kind', 'tall', 'happy', and 'helpful'.<br>2.
