Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-07-13 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays four pictures of different weather conditions on the board and asks a quick true or false question for each, such as 'Is it snowing in Malaysia?'.<br>2. Students like Muhammad Thaqif Danial answer by shouting 'True' or 'False' or using thumbs up and thumbs down signals.<br>3. Teacher confirms the answers and introduces the four main target words for the day: sunny, rainy, cloudy, and windy.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Weather Ranking Task</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher explains the characteristics of sunny, rainy, cloudy, and windy weather using simple flashcards and choral repetition to build confidence.<br>2. Teacher asks students to rank these four types of weather from 1 (best) to 4 (worst) based on their personal preference on a small slip of paper.<br>3. Students like Nur’irish Zara Amna share their top choice with the class using the sentence frame: 'I like sunny weather best because I can play football.'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Weather Pair Interview</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides a simple interview script on the board: 'A: What weather do you like? B: I like... A: Why? B: Because it is...'.<br>2. Students work in pairs to interview each other; for example, Nick Mikhael asks Santhiya about her favourite weather and records the answer.<br>3. Teacher walks around the classroom to assist weaker students with pronunciation and ensures everyone is using the provided sentence frames correctly.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher conducts a 3-2-1 closure activity where students must identify 3 weather words they learned and 2 questions they have about seasons.<br>2. Students like Muhammad Firdaus Alfandi share 1 opinion on which weather is the most difficult to walk in before the lesson ends.</p>

Reflection

The lesson was successful as students like Muhammad Thaqif Danial showed great enthusiasm during the ranking task. Nur Dayana Qhairena required some extra scaffolding with the sentence frames during the interview, but eventually succeeded with peer support.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-07-14 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays three weather icons (Sun, Rain, Snow) on the board and asks students to stand up if the weather is 'True' for today.<br>2. Teacher asks a quick quiz question: 'Does it snow in Malaysia?' and students respond with a loud 'No' or 'False'.<br>3. Teacher introduces the reading topic by showing a simple picture book cover about the four seasons.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Jigsaw Reading: Four Seasons</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides the class into four groups: Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter.<br>2. Each group receives a 3-sentence text, for example: 'It is summer. The sun is very hot. We go to the beach.'<br>3. Students practice reading their specific text together using choral repetition.<br>4. One student from each group moves to a new table to read their sentences to their peers, ensuring everyone hears about all four seasons.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Guided Sentence Frames for Reading</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher writes a short passage on the board: 'Today is a windy day. The leaves fall from the trees. I wear my jacket.'<br>2. Teacher highlights keywords like 'windy', 'leaves', and 'jacket' using different colored chalk.<br>3. Teacher models the pronunciation of the passage and has students repeat after her twice.<br>4. Teacher explains how to use the frame 'It is [weather] so I wear [clothing]' to understand the text better.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Small Group Discussion with Task Cards</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher hands out task cards containing a picture of a weather type and three simple sentences to each group.<br>2. Students like Muhammad Firdaus Alfandi and Nur Dayana Qhairena lead their groups to identify which sentence correctly describes the picture.<br>3. Groups must read the chosen sentence aloud to the teacher to confirm their understanding.<br>4. Students help each other match clothing items (e.g., umbrella, hat) to the weather described on their cards.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher distributes small slips of paper to serve as exit tickets.<br>2. Students write one simple sentence based on the lesson: 'Today the weather is [weather]' and submit it before leaving.</p>

Reflection

Most students successfully identified weather vocabulary in the short texts provided. Muhammad Thaqif Danial showed improved confidence during the choral reading, while Muslimahtul Ramadani required more scaffolding to complete her exit ticket sentence correctly.

Wednesday DLP — Reading Focus

R1E • 2026-07-15 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher presents a scenario where Ainnur Raudhah has exactly RM50 to buy supplies for a class party but her shopping list totals RM75.<br>2. Students are shown a projected list of items including '1kg Cake (RM40)', '10 Carbonated Drinks (RM15)', and '2 Bags of Chips (RM20)'.<br>3. Students must quickly discuss and suggest which items to remove or replace to help Ainnur Raudhah stay within her budget.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Receipt Analysis and Budgeting</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are divided into small groups and handed a Task Card containing a detailed supermarket receipt from a local store.<br>2. Kalvin Pillai and his group members must identify five specific pieces of information: the store name, the date of purchase, the subtotal, the tax amount, and the final total.<br>3. Groups discuss the 'Task Card' prompt: 'If the shopper had a RM10 voucher, what would the new total be?' and 'Which item was the most expensive?'<br>4. Students record their answers on the back of the task card to be shared later.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Comparative Shopping Jigsaw</strong></p>
<p>1. The class is split into four 'Expert' groups, each assigned a different short text describing price lists from a Wet Market, an Online Store, a Convenience Store, and a Wholesale Club.<br>2. Text A (Wet Market) describes: 'Fresh milk is sold for RM6.50 per liter. A loaf of white bread costs RM3.20. Grade A eggs are RM5.00 for ten.'<br>3. Khayla Dhia Jannah and other students in their expert groups analyze their specific text to find the prices of milk, bread, and eggs.<br>4. Students return to their 'Home' groups to share their data and fill in a comparative table.<br>5. Each Home group must write two sentences using connectors like 'however' or 'on the other hand' to compare the prices, such as 'The wet market is cheap; however, the convenience store is more expensive.'</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher distributes small slips of paper for an exit ticket activity.<br>2. Nur Zara Damia and her classmates write one sentence summarizing a tip for reading receipts or saving money before handing it to the teacher at the door.</p>

Reflection

The lesson was effective as Ainnur Raudhah and Kalvin Pillai successfully managed the budgeting scenario with logical reasoning. Khayla Dhia Jannah showed significant improvement in using contrastive connectors during the jigsaw sharing session.
