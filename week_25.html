Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher plays a short 30-second audio clip of a child excitedly talking about receiving a new teddy bear for their birthday.<br>2. <p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary and Sentence Scaffolding</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces key vocabulary: 'robot', 'storybook', 'bicycle', 'shiny', and 'fast' using picture cards.<br>2. Teacher conducts choral repetition of the words to ensure correct pronunciation.<br>3. Teacher introduces sentence frames on the board: 'My favourite thing is a...', 'It is [color/size]', and 'I like it because it is...'.</p>

<p><strong>Jigsaw Reading: Friends and Their Toys</strong></p>

Students move to expert groups to discuss their specific text, then return to home groups to teach their teammates what they read using the sentence frames.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p>1. Students work in pairs, such as Muslimahtul Ramadani and Nick Mikhael bin Hendra Cipta.<br>2. Student B reads the description aloud while Student A listens and writes the correct name under each picture.<br>4. Teacher monitors Muhammad Syahir Haikal and Muhammad Thaqif Danial, providing extra guidance with the reading prompts.<br>5. Pairs swap roles with a new set of items (a purple doll and a brown drum) to practice both reading and listening.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher asks students to complete a '3-2-1' exit ticket: write 3 new words learned, 2 questions about the story characters, and 1 opinion on which toy was the best.<br>2. Nur Dayana Qhairena shares her opinion with the class, stating why she prefers the storybook over the bicycle.</p>

Reflection

Muhamad Harith Aqasha showed good progress in identifying the main idea of the short passage. Muslimahtul Ramadani and Nick Mikhael bin Hendra Cipta successfully completed the info-gap task with minimal teacher intervention, showing improved confidence in reading simple sentences.

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Group Task Card Brainstorming</strong></p>
<p>1. Students sit in small groups and receive a task card featuring a picture of a common gift like a bicycle or a teddy bear.<br>2. Muhammad Syahir Haikal and his group members discuss who they would give the card to, such as 'Mom' or 'Dad'.<br>3. Each student writes a draft of their thank-you card on a worksheet, using the group's task card as a visual reference for vocabulary.</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>

Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1M • 2026-07-23 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.5 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Display a silhouette or partial photo of a famous person on the screen. Ask: "Who is this? How would you describe them?"<br>2. Elicit 3-4 describing words from students (e.g., tall, kind, famous). Write them on the board.<br>3. Tell students: "Today, you will write a full description of a person -- just like a character profile."</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Structure of a Character Description</strong></p>
<p>1. Show a model paragraph describing a character (e.g. a teacher). Highlight four parts: name/introduction, appearance, personality, and interesting facts.<br>2. Point out connecting words: <b>and</b>, <b>but</b>, <b>because</b>, <b>also</b>. Circle reference pronouns: <b>he</b>, <b>she</b>, <b>they</b>, <b>his</b>, <b>her</b>.<br>3. Write sentence frames on the board: "He/She is ... because ...", "... also ...", "... but ...".<br>4. Ask Choye Kai En: "If you describe your best friend, what would you say first?" Then ask Auni: "What about personality words?"</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Guided Writing -- Describe a Classmate</strong></p>
<p>1. Pair students. Each pair chooses one person to describe (a classmate, a teacher, or a family member).<br>2. Provide a planning sheet with four sections: Name & Introduction, Appearance, Personality, Interesting Fact. Students jot 2-3 points per section.<br>3. Students write 1-2 paragraphs using the sentence frames and connectors. Target: at least 6 sentences with 2 coordinating conjunctions and 2 reference pronouns.<br>4. Circulate and give individual feedback. Check Afif Irsyaduddin's use of 'because' and Ahmad Harraz' pronoun consistency.<br>5. Early finishers add an extra sentence about 'What I admire about this person'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Peer Review & Gallery Walk</strong></p>
<p>1. Students swap papers with another pair. They read and underline: one colour for conjunctions, another for reference pronouns.<br>2. Each pair gives one positive comment and one suggestion for improvement.<br>3. Volunteers read their descriptions aloud. Class guesses who is being described based on the details.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Recap: Ask 2-3 students to share one sentence from their description.<br>2. Quick quiz: Show 3 sentences with missing connectors/pronouns. Students complete orally.<br>3. Exit ticket: Write one sentence describing someone in your family using 'because'.</p>
