Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-07-27 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher tells students that a new friend is joining the class and we need to tell them our favourite things to make them feel welcome.<br>2. Teacher asks students to think about how they can explain what they like using very short sentences.<br>3. Students suggest simple words like 'blue' or 'cat' to help solve the problem of introducing themselves.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Sentence Frame Introduction</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher shows flashcards of primary colours, numbers 1--10, and common objects like balls and cats.<br>2. Teacher models the sentence frame 'I like [colour/number/object]' using clear gestures.<br>3. Students engage in choral repetition of the frame to familiarise themselves with the rhythm of the sentence.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Task Card Group Discussion</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides students into small groups and provides each group with a task card containing images of various items.<br>2. Students take turns pointing to an item on their card and saying 'I like [item]' to their group members.<br>3. Group members provide a 'high-five' to their peers after each successful sentence is spoken.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Jigsaw Reading and Sharing</strong></p>
<p>1. Group A reads a short text: 'Adam likes blue. He has a blue hat. Blue is nice.'<br>2. Group B reads a short text: 'Maya likes five. She has five books. Five is a lucky number.'<br>3. Students from Group A pair with Group B to tell their new partner what Adam or Maya likes using the frame 'Adam/Maya likes...'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher says several sentences such as 'I like red' or 'I likes green'.<br>2. Students give a thumbs up if the sentence sounds correct and a thumbs down if they hear a mistake.</p>

Reflection

Dharshayani A/P Paul and Mohamad Ryyan bin Roslan successfully completed the jigsaw speaking task with minimal errors. Nur Dayana Qhairena showed improvement in identifying colours but still needs help with the 'I like' sentence frame.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-07-28 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher presents a 'Lost Pet' poster that is missing a picture of the animal.<br>2. Teacher explains that the owner is very sad and needs help describing the pet to the neighbors.<br>3. Students brainstorm simple words like 'big', 'small', or 'brown' to help the owner find the pet.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Pet Profile Info-Gap</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides students into pairs and gives each student a different Pet Profile card.<br>2. Student A reads a short text: 'The cat is thin. It is grey.'<br>3. Student B reads a short text: 'The rabbit is fat. It is white.'<br>4. Students ask each other simple questions to fill in a missing information table about the pets' sizes and colors.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pet Show Simulation</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher sets up a classroom 'Pet Show' where students act as judges and owners.<br>2. Students use a reading strip with a sentence frame: 'This is my pet. It is [adjective] and [color].'<br>3. Each student reads their strip aloud to the 'judges' while showing a picture of the animal.<br>4. Judges listen and circle the correct adjective they heard on a score sheet.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. One student stands up and summarizes the different animals and adjectives learned during the lesson.<br>2. Other students add more adjectives or animal names to the summary to help their classmate.</p>

Reflection

Mohamad Ryyan bin Roslan showed great progress in reading the sentence frames aloud. Nur Dayana Qhairena was able to identify the correct adjectives during the info-gap activity with very little guidance.

Friday DLP — Writing Focus

J1D • 2026-07-31 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays three simple sentences about toys on the board.<br>2. Students shout "True" or "False" based on the pictures shown next to each sentence.<br>3. Teacher confirms the answers to review vocabulary such as "ball", "doll", and "bike".</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Sentence Frames for Writing</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces simple sentence frames: "My favourite thing is a..." and "It is (colour)."<br>2. Students repeat the frames chorally to build confidence and familiarity.<br>3. Teacher models filling in the blanks using a picture of a blue car on the board.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Jigsaw Reading Task</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides the class into four groups, each receiving a different short text about a favourite object.<br>2. Groups read their specific text, such as "This is a kite. It is green. It is big."<br>3. Students move to meet peers from other groups to share one detail about their assigned text.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Small Group Discussion and Writing</strong></p>
<p>1. Groups receive task cards featuring pictures of a train, a robot, and a puzzle.<br>2. Students use the task cards to discuss which item they like best in their small groups.<br>3. Each student writes two simple sentences about their chosen item in their exercise books.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher distributes small slips of paper to every student for the exit ticket.<br>2. Students write one sentence about their favourite thing and hand it to the teacher before leaving.</p>

Reflection

Dharshayani and Santhiya successfully completed their writing tasks with minimal support. Mohamad Ryyan and Muhammad Afiq Ziyad showed progress in using the sentence frames correctly during the group activity.

Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1E • 2026-07-27 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher asks the provocative question: 'If you could only eat one food for the rest of your life, would you choose health or taste?'<br>2. Students think individually for one minute then discuss their choice and reasoning with a partner.<br>3. Selected pairs share their opinions with the class using connectors like 'because' and 'furthermore' to justify their choice.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Jigsaw Reading: The Four Pillars of Nutrition</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are divided into four 'expert' groups, each reading a short text about a specific food category: Proteins, Carbohydrates, Healthy Fats, or Vitamins.<br>2. Each text describes the benefits and examples of that category in three to five sentences.<br>3. Students return to their original home groups to teach their members about their specific category.<br>4. Group members must take notes on the categories they did not read about to complete a full nutritional profile.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Info-Gap: The Healthy Cafe Interview</strong></p>
<p>1. Students work in pairs where Student A has a menu with prices but missing health ratings, and Student B has health ratings but missing prices.<br>2. Students must interview each other using target phrases like 'How healthy is the...' and 'How much does the... cost?' to complete their menus.<br>3. Once the menus are complete, pairs must negotiate and choose the best meal based on both cost and health benefits.<br>4. Pairs present their final choice to the class with a brief explanation.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher shows a mysterious, zoomed-in photo of a rare tropical 'superfood' without revealing its name.<br>2. Teacher asks if anyone knows how this fruit can boost the immune system, promising the answer in the next lesson.</p>

Reflection

Celine Yan-Xin Phee and Huzaifah demonstrated high fluency during the info-gap activity by using extended sentences. Deni Aago and Nur Adni Irdina showed great improvement in using logical connectors when explaining their nutritional profiles.

Wednesday DLP — Reading Focus

R1E • 2026-07-29 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students move around the classroom to view five posters displaying various healthy and unhealthy meal sets.<br>2. Students identify and write down one key ingredient from each poster on a small slip of paper.<br>3. Teacher facilitates a brief discussion on which meal provides the most energy based on the ingredients found.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Menu Comparison Text Analysis</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher presents a reading text comparing two food outlets: 'The Green Deli' and 'Sugar Shack'.<br>2. The text highlights that 'The Green Deli' serves lean proteins and whole grains, whereas 'Sugar Shack' focuses on processed carbs.<br>3. Teacher explains the use of comparative connectors like 'on the other hand' and 'similarly' found in the text.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Nutritional Info-Gap</strong></p>
<p>1. Students work in pairs where Student A has the fiber content of the meals and Student B has the sugar content.<br>2. They must communicate in English to exchange data and complete a full nutritional profile table for both outlets.<br>3. Pairs verify their answers against the original reading text to ensure accuracy.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Healthy Cafe Simulation</strong></p>
<p>1. Students act out a simulation where a customer asks a waiter for the healthiest options on the menu.<br>2. The waiter must use information from the reading text to justify why the grilled chicken is better than the fried wings.<br>3. Students switch roles to ensure everyone practices using the vocabulary from the comprehension text.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. One student provides a summary of the health benefits associated with the food items discussed in the lesson.<br>2. Other students add additional points regarding the importance of reading nutritional labels.</p>

Reflection

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Paragraph Structure and Connectors</strong></p>

Wednesday DLP — Speaking Focus

R1M • 2026-07-29 • CS 2.4 / LS 2.4.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides three clues about a digital entity: 'I can process millions of words', 'I can act as a personal tutor', and 'I am built on neural networks'.<br>2. Students compete in groups to guess the entity, eventually identifying it as 'Artificial Intelligence' or 'AI'.<br>3. Teacher explains that today students will use AI to plan the structure of their own English eCourseware.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Jigsaw Reading: eCourseware Components</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are divided into four home groups, each receiving a text about a different component: 'Interactive Quizzes', 'Grammar Modules', 'Dialogue Scripts', or 'User Interface'.<br>2. The text for 'Interactive Quizzes' explains that they must include multiple-choice questions with immediate AI-generated feedback.<br>3. Students move to expert groups to master their component before returning to their home groups to teach their peers.<br>4. Home groups negotiate which components are most essential for their specific target audience using spoken English.<br>5. Groups finalize a list of four main sections for their eCourseware.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Info-Gap: Role and Tool Matching</strong></p>
<p>1. In pairs, Student A receives a card listing AI tool strengths (e.g., ChatGPT for creative scripts, DeepSeek for logical structuring).<br>2. Student B receives a card listing project roles (e.g., Prompt Engineer, Content Editor, UI Designer) and their specific needs.<br>3. Pairs must speak to each other to match the correct AI tool to the specific role and eCourseware section.<br>4. Students negotiate and assign roles within their larger groups based on the matched tools.<br>5. Each group orally presents their chosen structure and assigned roles to the class.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher asks the class if they believe an AI can generate a perfect English quiz in under ten seconds.<br>2. Teacher reveals that in the next lesson, they will test their prompts against the AI to see if the content is truly accurate.</p>

Friday DLP — Writing Focus

J2D • 2026-07-31 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher plays a 30-second audio clip of a tourist sounding lost and asking for help in a busy city.<br>2. Students share their reactions to the clip and identify the problem the person is facing.<br>3. Teacher introduces the lesson goal: writing simple directions to help people find places in town.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K2E • 2026-07-30 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.3 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher shows three short TikTok-style video clips of people turning hobbies into careers (baking → pastry chef, gaming → esports commentator, sketching → animator).<br>2. Students raise hands to say which hobby-career link surprised them most.<br>3. Teacher frames the writing task: you will write a short summary explaining how a hobby connects to a future dream career.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Text Model - Hobby to Career</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher projects a model text: 'Khairul loves fixing old motorbikes. This hobby taught him problem-solving and patience. He now dreams of being a mechanical engineer.'<br>2. Class identifies key parts: hobby (fixing bikes), skills gained (problem-solving, patience), career (mechanical engineer).<br>3. Teacher highlights linking phrases: because of this hobby, this taught me, this connects to.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pair Idea Web - Connect the Dots</strong></p>
<p>1. In pairs, students draw a simple 2-circle Venn diagram: left circle = hobby, right circle = dream job, overlap = skills shared.<br>2. Pairs discuss what skills their hobby builds and brainstorm which career fits.<br>3. Each pair shares one connection with the class. Teacher gives oral feedback on reasoning quality.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Independent Writing - Paragraph Draft</strong></p>
<p>1. Students write a 4-5 sentence paragraph: state hobby → describe skills gained → explain career link → give a reason.<br>2. Teacher circulates, checks sentence frames and connector use (because, therefore, as a result).<br>3. Early finishers swap paragraphs with a partner and underline the best linking phrase used.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Exit ticket: Students write one new linking phrase they used today that they had not used before.<br>2. Teacher previews next week: we will peer-edit these paragraphs and publish them as a class blog post.</p>
