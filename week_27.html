Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-08-03 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher asks a provocative question: "If you could only visit one place in school for the rest of the year, would you choose the Canteen or the Library?"<br>2. Students think individually for 30 seconds, then pair up to share their choice using the frame: "I choose the... because I like..."<br>3. Teacher invites two pairs to share their partner's answer with the whole class to check for understanding.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Exam-Style WH-Questions and Sentence Frames</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces three common exam questions: "Where is the place?", "What can you do there?", and "Who can you see there?"<br>2. Teacher displays simple sentence frames: "The [place] is next to the...", "I can [verb] there.", and "I can see the [person]."<br>3. Students practice choral repetition of these frames to ensure correct word order and pronunciation.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Information Jigsaw Sharing</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are divided into groups where each member is given a different short text (3 sentences) about either the Canteen, the Library, or the Science Lab.<br>2. Each student reads their text silently and then takes turns "briefing" their group members by answering the three WH-questions from the explanation phase.<br>3. Peers use a simple peer-assessment checklist (1-3 stars) to score the speaker on using complete sentences and correct vocabulary.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher asks: "What if there was a secret room hidden behind the principal's office that no one knows about?"<br>2. Teacher tells students that in the next lesson, they will learn the vocabulary to describe that secret room and write a short story about it.</p>

Reflection

Mohamad Wafy Hau and Muslimahtul Ramadani showed great improvement in using the sentence frames during the jigsaw activity. Muhammad Izzudin and Nur Amni Awatif required more scaffolding but successfully answered the 'Where' questions by the end of the session.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-08-04 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher asks a provocative question: "If our school library was closed forever, where would you find books to read?"<br>2. Students think individually, discuss their ideas with a partner, and then share their answers with the class.<br>3. Teacher links the discussion to the importance of identifying key details about school places in reading exams.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Finding Keywords and Main Ideas</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays a short text: "The school canteen is very busy. Students buy food during recess. It is located near the hall."<br>2. Teacher explains that the main idea is usually at the start and models underlining keywords like "canteen", "food", and "near the hall".<br>3. Teacher shows how these keywords help answer exam questions about location and purpose.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Exam Detective Simulation</strong></p>
<p>1. Pairs participate in a simulation where one student acts as the "Exam Candidate" and the other as the "Examiner".<br>2. The "Exam Candidate" reads this passage: "The science lab is a big room. Students do experiments here. They must wear lab coats."<br>3. The "Examiner" asks two exam-style questions: "Q1: What is the text about? (A. Experiments, B. The science lab). Q2: What must students wear? (Answer: Lab coats)."<br>4. Students swap roles to practice extracting key details from the short text.<br>5. Teacher moves between pairs to provide choral repetition of the correct answers for scaffolding.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher conducts a quick quiz by reading statements about the science lab text.<br>2. Students give a thumbs-up if the statement is true (e.g., "Students do experiments") or a thumbs-down if it is false (e.g., "The lab is a small room").</p>

Reflection

Mohamad Wafy Hau and Muhamad Harith Aqasha successfully identified the main idea in the passage during the simulation. Muslimahtul Ramadani showed significant improvement in answering the WH-question correctly with minimal teacher prompting.

Friday DLP — Writing Focus

J1D • 2026-08-07 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher plays a 30-second audio clip featuring the sounds of a school bell, children playing, and a teacher speaking.<br>2. Students listen and identify the school locations they hear in the clip.<br>3. Teacher explains that identifying these places will help them in the guided writing section of their upcoming exam.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Guided Writing Exam Requirements</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays an exam-style paragraph about a school library with missing words and punctuation errors.<br>2. Teacher explains how to fill in the blanks using a provided word bank and emphasizes the use of capital letters for place names.<br>3. Teacher provides a checklist for students: Vocabulary, Capitalization, and Punctuation (VCP).</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>School Places Jigsaw Reading</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are split into home groups and given different short texts about the canteen, the field, or the computer lab.<br>2. Students move to expert groups to discuss their specific text and identify three key details for their guided writing task.<br>3. Students return to home groups to share information and complete a full exam-style paragraph on a worksheet using the gathered details.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students complete a 3-2-1 activity by writing 3 school places they can name, 2 punctuation rules, and 1 opinion about the activity.<br>2. Teacher reviews a few responses to clarify common punctuation mistakes before the lesson ends.</p>

Reflection

Muhamad Harith Aqasha and Muslimahtul Ramadani showed great accuracy in using capital letters during the jigsaw activity. Muhammad Thaqif Danial required additional scaffolding with the word bank but successfully completed the guided paragraph.

Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1E • 2026-08-03 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher places four images of daily activities around the room with prompts such as --What is your favorite time of day?--<br>2. Students move in pairs to each station, taking turns to answer the prompts using complete sentences within a 30-second limit per station.<br>3. Teacher monitors the gallery walk and notes down interesting vocabulary used by students to be highlighted later.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Structuring Responses with Reasons</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces the --Point + Reason + Example-- strategy for answering personal response questions in speaking tests.<br>2. Teacher demonstrates how to use connectors like --because-- and --since-- to extend answers beyond a single word.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>The Daily Routine Info-Gap</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are paired; Student A receives a partial routine for a fictional character, while Student B receives the missing details.<br>2. Students must ask each other specific questions to fill the gaps, such as --What does the character do after school and why?--<br>3. Pairs then transition to asking each other real exam questions about their own lives, applying the --Point + Reason-- structure.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students spend two minutes writing a brief reflection on which part of the speaking exam they feel most confident about.<br>2. Students identify one specific connector they will try to use in their next speaking practice to improve their score.</p>

Reflection

Ainnur Raudhah and Chai Zi Jian showed great independence in using complex connectors during the info-gap activity. Mohamad Aqil Fayyadh and Nur Anis Dzakirah successfully provided detailed reasons for their daily choices, meeting the B1 target.

Wednesday DLP — Reading Focus

R1E • 2026-08-05 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides three cryptic clues about a person's hidden daily routine, such as "I carry a heavy bag but I am not a traveler."<br>2. Students guess the profession or activity based on these indirect clues to practice the concept of inferencing.<br>3. Teacher introduces the lesson objective of finding implied information in reading texts.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Inferencing and Implied Meaning</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher explains that inferencing involves using text clues and personal logic to understand what is not explicitly written.<br>2. Teacher presents an exam-style passage: "Ainnur looks at the dark clouds and sighs. She puts on her raincoat and grabs her sturdy umbrella before heading to the bus stop."<br>3. Teacher asks two exam questions: "Q1: What is the weather like? Q2: How does Ainnur feel about the weather?"<br>4. Teacher demonstrates how to use the words "dark clouds" and "sighs" to infer it is about to rain and she is unhappy.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Detective Interview Simulation</strong></p>
<p>1. Students work in pairs where one acts as a 'Daily Life Detective' and the other as a 'Suspect' describing their day using only hints.<br>2. The 'Suspect' says things like "My hands are always covered in flour by 6 AM," and the 'Detective' must infer the job (baker).<br>3. The 'Detective' records the clues and the final inference in a table to simulate an exam-style evidence-gathering task.<br>4. Pairs switch roles to ensure both practice identifying implied meanings in spoken and written contexts.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher reads out three statements regarding the characters in the earlier passage.<br>2. Students give a 'thumbs-up' if the statement is a valid inference based on evidence or a 'thumbs-down' if it is a wild guess.</p>

Reflection

Khayla Dhia Jannah and Chai Zi Jian showed excellent critical thinking when explaining their inferences during the simulation. Low Mei Yik and Muhammad Ammar Ziqri successfully identified the implied information in the exam-style passage with minimal guidance.

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1E • 2026-08-06 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.5 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher presents a scenario where a student loses marks in an exam because their paragraph about daily life is a disorganized wall of text that the examiner cannot follow.<br>2. Students in pairs brainstorm three ways to fix the text to make it clearer and more professional for a pen-pal or an examiner.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>The Burger Model for Paragraph Writing</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces the 'Burger Model' structure: Topic Sentence (introduction), Supporting Details (daily activities with time), and Concluding Sentence (feelings/summary).<br>2. Teacher highlights the importance of cohesive devices such as 'Furthermore' and 'In addition' to achieve higher marks in the Organisation category of the exam.<br>3. Teacher displays the marking criteria focus: Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, and Language.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Gallery Walk: Daily Life Paragraphs</strong></p>
<p>1. Students write a 60--80 word paragraph about their typical school day following the Burger Model on A3 paper.<br>2. Students display their work around the classroom and move in groups to read their peers' writing.<br>3. Students use sticky notes to provide peer feedback specifically on whether the paragraph has a clear topic sentence and a conclusion.<br>4. Students return to their work to make final corrections based on the feedback received.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students complete a 3-2-1 exit slip: write 3 things learned about paragraph structure, 2 questions about exam requirements, and 1 opinion on the gallery walk activity.<br>2. Teacher collects the slips to identify common misconceptions for the next review session.</p>

Reflection

Chai Zi Jian and Krettisha Nair showed high proficiency by using advanced connectors like 'subsequently' in their writing. Meanwhile, Mohamad Aqil Fayyadh and Nur Zara Damia required extra guidance to ensure their concluding sentences reflected the main topic rather than adding new information.

<p>1. Teacher displays three zoomed-in, blurred images of hobby-related items such as a camera lens, a chess piece, and a hiking boot.<br>2. Students work in pairs to predict the hobbies and use phrases like --I reckon it is-- or --It might be-- to justify their guesses.</p>

Thursday DLP — Reading Focus

K2D • 2026-08-06 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1 
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher describes a scenario where a tourist is hungry but only has 3 minutes to find a nut-free meal on a large menu before their bus leaves.<br>2. Teacher asks students to suggest ways to find the information quickly without reading every single word.<br>3. Students identify that looking for keywords like "nuts" or "price" is the fastest way to solve the problem.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Scanning and Inferring Techniques</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher explains that scanning means moving eyes quickly to find specific information like prices (RM) or food names.<br>2. Teacher introduces a word bank: poultry (meat), beverages (drinks), and vegetarian (no meat) to help with context.<br>3. Teacher demonstrates inferring meaning by explaining that if a dish is in the "Vegetarian" section, it is safe for people who do not eat meat.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Exam-Style Gallery Walk</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher places a short text at four stations: "Healthy Cafe Menu. We serve fresh orange juice for RM5. Our chicken salad is very popular. It has no nuts. Vegetarians can try our mushroom soup."<br>2. In groups, students must answer two exam-style questions: "Q1: How much is the juice?" and "Q2: Can someone with a nut allergy eat the salad?"<br>3. Groups move to each station to write their answers on a mahjong paper.<br>4. Students use sticky notes to leave peer feedback: a tick for correct answers or a question mark if they disagree.<br>5. Teacher facilitates a brief discussion on why "no nuts" implies safety for allergy sufferers.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students complete a 3-2-1 exit ticket: write 3 things learned about scanning, 2 questions about the menu, and 1 opinion on the food.<br>2. Teacher summarizes the lesson by linking scanning skills to time management during the actual English exam.</p>

Reflection

Adriesya Shahzuelia and Izzara Safiya showed great improvement in scanning for prices during the gallery walk. However, Muhammad Farish Eiman still needs more guidance on inferring meanings of complex vocabulary.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher presents a scenario where a foreign friend is visiting Malaysia and needs help choosing a healthy meal from a menu with no pictures.<br>2.
