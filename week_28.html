Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-08-10 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 cat · dog · fish · bird · fur · tail · friendly · look after

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'Animals and Pets'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher places large flashcards of a cat, dog, fish, and bird at different corners of the classroom to create a gallery walk.<br>2. Students like Muhammad Afiq Ziyad and Nur Dayana Qhairena walk to each card and whisper the name of the animal to their partner.<br>3. Teacher rings a bell and students must make the sound of the animal they are currently standing near before returning to their seats.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary Introduction and Drill</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher writes the unit word bank on the board: cat, dog, fish, bird, fur, tail, friendly, look after.<br>2. Teacher models the pronunciation of each word and students engage in choral repetition.<br>3. Teacher uses simple gestures -- such as stroking an imaginary pet for 'fur' -- to help students understand the meanings.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pet Trait Info-Gap</strong></p>
<p>1. Students are divided into pairs where Student A receives a picture of an animal and Student B receives a list of traits like 'fur' or 'tail'.<br>2. Student A asks 'What does my pet have?' and Student B must look at their list to provide the matching trait.<br>3. Students swap roles using different animal cards to ensure they practice all words in the unit bank.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pet Shop Simulation</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher sets up a corner of the room as a pet shop with students like Santhiya and Muhammad Nazrin acting as shopkeepers.<br>2. Other students act as customers and use the sentence frame 'I want a [animal] that is [friendly].'<br>3. The shopkeeper hands over a toy or card and says 'Please look after this [animal]' to practice the target phrase.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students participate in an individual reflection by pointing to a 'smiley face' if they found the animal names easy or a 'thinking face' if words like 'friendly' were hard.<br>2. Teacher provides a curiosity hook by telling students that in the next session, they will find out what these specific pets like to eat.</p>

Reflection

Santhiya and Muhammad Nazrin demonstrated good confidence when using the sentence frames during the simulation. Muhammad Afiq Ziyad required additional scaffolding with the word 'friendly' but succeeded after choral repetition.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-08-11 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1
Session 2 of 3 cat · dog · fish · bird · fur · tail · friendly · look after

SECOND session of the 3-session unit on 'Animals and Pets'. Builds on Session 1 (Animal vocabulary -- matching and sounds game). Must OPEN with a recap of the unit vocabulary from Session 1.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher greets the class and asks students to recall the animal sounds and names from the previous session to activate prior knowledge.<br>2. Students participate in a 5-question mini-quiz on the board where they match animal names like cat and dog to physical features like fur and tail.<br>3. Students swap their quiz sheets with a partner to self-check the answers as the teacher reveals the correct matches.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Animal Description Gallery Walk</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays four simple texts around the room: 'This is a cat. It has fur. It is friendly.', 'This is a dog. It has a tail.', 'This is a fish. It is a pet.', 'This is a bird. We look after it.'<br>2. Teacher leads the class in choral repetition of each text to practice pronunciation and rhythm.<br>3. Students walk in pairs to each station and use sticky notes to leave a 'smiley face' next to the word 'friendly' and a 'star' next to 'look after' whenever they find them.<br>4. Dharshayani A/P Paul and Muhammad Firdaus Alfandi lead their respective groups to identify the word 'fur' in the descriptions.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Word Bank Board Relay</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides the class into two teams and writes incomplete sentence frames on the board using the unit word bank.<br>2. Teacher calls out a feature such as 'tail' or 'friendly', and the first student from each team races to complete the sentence 'A dog is --.' or 'A cat has a --.'<br>3. Muhammad Nazrin and Nur Dayana Qhairena compete to correctly write the phrase 'look after' in the sentence frame 'We -- -- our pets.'<br>4. The teacher facilitates a final choral reading of all the completed sentences on the board to consolidate reading comprehension.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher summarizes the lesson by reviewing the key vocabulary: cat, dog, fish, bird, fur, tail, friendly, and look after.<br>2. Teacher asks, 'What if your pet could talk to you?' to provide a curiosity hook for the final session where students will create a speaking pet project.</p>

Reflection

Muhammad Nazrin and Nur Dayana Qhairena showed great enthusiasm during the relay race and successfully used the phrase 'look after'. Dharshayani A/P Paul demonstrated improved confidence in reading simple sentences during the gallery walk activity.

Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1E • 2026-08-10 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 big · busy · quiet · beautiful · noisy · clean · crowded · there is

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'Describing Places'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides three clues about a famous place, for example, 'It is a place where many people go to shop' and 'It is very noisy and crowded'.<br>2. Students like Huzaifah and Jayamitthra raise their hands to guess the name of the place based on the descriptive clues provided.<br>3. Teacher introduces the lesson topic by explaining that today they will learn eight specific words to describe different places accurately.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary and Sentence Structure Introduction</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays the unit word bank on the whiteboard: big, busy, quiet, beautiful, noisy, clean, crowded, and the phrase 'there is'.<br>2. Teacher explains how to combine 'there is' with adjectives to form descriptive sentences, such as 'There is a big park'.<br>3. Teacher conducts a choral repetition drill to ensure students like Kalvin Pillai and Keerthikan can pronounce the adjectives with the correct stress and intonation.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Travel Agent Simulation</strong></p>
<p>1. Students work in pairs to perform a role-play where one person is a travel agent and the other is a tourist looking for a holiday spot.<br>2. The tourist, like Celine Yan-Xin Phee, asks about a place, while the agent, like Deni Aago, describes it using at least three adjectives from the word bank.<br>3. Students are encouraged to use connectors like 'and' or 'but' to link their descriptions, for example, 'The city is busy but it is very clean'.<br>4. Teacher moves around the classroom to facilitate the conversations and provide immediate feedback on sentence structure.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Descriptive Gallery Walk</strong></p>
<p>1. Groups of four create a quick 'Place Profile' on a mahjong paper, drawing a location and writing three sentences using the target vocabulary.<br>2. Groups paste their work on the walls and students walk around to read the descriptions written by their classmates.<br>3. Students use sticky notes to leave peer feedback, focusing on whether their friends used the adjectives correctly.<br>4. DhiviniesH Rao and Nur Adni Irdina lead their respective groups in presenting one interesting feedback they received from their peers.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students complete an exit ticket by writing one full sentence about their hometown using the structure 'There is...' and one adjective from today's list.<br>2. Teacher collects the tickets and hints that in the next session, they will use these exact words to build a longer travel blog, so they must remember the definitions.</p>

Reflection

Adzin Danish showed significant progress in his speaking confidence by using the word 'crowded' correctly during the simulation. Nur Qisya Anggun and Nur Madinah Musfirah were very active during the gallery walk, providing detailed feedback to other groups on their use of 'beautiful' and 'quiet'.

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Reading Strategy: Main Idea and Supporting Details</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays a short article (3-5 sentences) titled "Why Our School Should Go Green" and reads it aloud once.<br>2. Teacher models finding the main idea by asking "What is the article mostly about?" and underlining the key sentence.<br>3. Teacher explains that supporting details give reasons, examples or facts, and circles two examples in the text.<br>4. Teacher writes the sentence frames "The main idea is that..." and "One supporting detail is that..." on the board.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Jigsaw Reading: Protecting Our Environment</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides the class into groups of four and gives each group one of two short texts: "Cutting Down on Plastic" or "Saving Water at School".<br>2. Each group reads its text and underlines the main idea sentence and two supporting details.<br>3. Groups complete a simple table using the frames "Main idea: ...", "Detail 1: ...", "Detail 2: ...".<br>4. One representative from each group presents their table while the teacher notes which unit words were reused (plastic, recycle, protect, pollution).</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Gallery Walk with Peer Feedback</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher pins the completed tables around the room.<br>2. Pupils walk in pairs to read the other groups' tables and stick a small note with a question or a tick if the main idea is clear.<br>3. Teacher leads a whole-class review and asks pupils to explain, in their own words, one supporting detail from a text they did not read.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher distributes exit tickets and asks pupils to complete: "The main idea is that..." and "One detail is...".<br>2. Teacher ends with a curiosity hook: "Next session, you will write your own proposal to make our school go green. Start thinking: what is one change you would suggest?"</p>
