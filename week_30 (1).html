Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-08-24 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 favourite · colours · numbers · toy · pet · I like · because · mine

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'My Favourite Things'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Project interactive whiteboard slides showing mystery boxes and have pupils guess the hidden objects related to a toy and a pet.<br>2. Introduce the core question, what is your favourite thing, and model the target response using the word favourite, colours, and numbers.<br>3. Pair stronger pupils with A0-A1 learners, using visual flashcards to help lower-proficiency pupils repeat simple sentences like I like cats.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocab Pop-Up and Choral Drilling</strong></p>
<p>1. Display the unit word bank on the interactive whiteboard, covering favourite, colours, numbers, toy, pet, I like, because, and mine.<br>2. Lead the class in choral repetition of each word, ensuring correct pronunciation and checking for understanding.<br>3. Provide a picture card matching sheet for A0-A1 learners to point and match vocabulary while peers do oral spelling.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Interactive Slide Pass the Mic</strong></p>
<p>1. Show an interactive slide with various items categorized by colours and numbers, prompting pupils to point out their favourite.<br>2. Call on pupils to stand and state one sentence using I like and because, explaining their choice briefly.<br>3. Allow lower-proficiency pupils to use a sentence frame card, reading aloud with a supportive partner to avoid public pressure.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Stand and Swap Speaking Carousel</strong></p>
<p>1. Instruct pupils to mingle in the classroom with a mini whiteboard, asking classmates about their favourite toy or pet.<br>2. Require pupils to respond using because and mine when identifying their own possessions during the exchange.<br>3. Circulate the room to provide oral prompts for struggling speakers, ensuring all learners participate without worksheet reliance.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Conduct a whole class exit ticket activity by asking pupils to call out one new word they learned today before packing up.<br>2. Summarize the target phrases on the board, praising active participation across all proficiency levels.</p>

Reflection

The interactive whiteboard slides kept Muhammad Afiq Ziyad and Nur Amni Awatif engaged, avoiding the late-afternoon sleepiness seen in previous stand-up games. However, Muhammad Izzudin and Santhiya A/P Subramaniam showed resistance during the speaking carousel because they dislike forced differentiation tasks that single them out. Dharshayani A/P Paul and Nick Mikhael Bin Hendra Cipta easily mastered the vocabulary, while Muslimahtul Ramadani and Muhammad Nazrin benefited from the sentence frame cards to build their speaking confidence.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-08-25 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1
Session 2 of 3 favourite · colours · numbers · toy · pet · I like · because · mine

SECOND session of the 3-session unit on 'My Favourite Things'. Builds on Session 1 (Review favourite vocab -- memory game). Must OPEN with a recap of the unit vocabulary from Session 1.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Welcome pupils back to the second session of our unit on My Favourite Things, and immediately review the vocabulary and memory game from Session 1.<br>2. Project the word bank on the interactive whiteboard containing favourite, colours, numbers, toy, pet, I like, because, and mine.<br>3. Call on volunteers to match flashcards to the correct terms while lower-proficiency learners use picture prompts for support.<br>4. Briefly check understanding by asking pupils to state one item from the word bank in a simple sentence.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>

<p><strong>Interactive Word and Phrase Matching</strong></p>
<p>1. Display interactive whiteboard slides featuring short descriptive sentences about pets and toys linked to colours and numbers.<br>2. Read each sentence aloud and guide the class to identify key terms like favourite and because.<br>3. Invite higher-proficiency pupils to complete matching tasks on the board, while A0-A1 learners receive simplified matching cards with visual cues.<br>4. Check answers as a class and reinforce correct pronunciation.</p>

<p><strong>Scaffolded Information Transfer</strong></p>
<p>1. Distribute a short reading text where characters describe their favourite things, incorporating words such as toy, pet, and mine.<br>2. Direct pupils to complete an information transfer worksheet extracting specific details into a simple table.<br>3. Provide a scaffolded worksheet version with sentence starters for lower-proficiency pupils to ensure access without feeling segregated.<br>4. Monitor progress closely and offer targeted guidance to pupils who need extra support.</p>

<p><strong>Reading Comprehension Check</strong></p>
<p>1. Conduct a rapid whole-class check using a digital quiz projected on the screen to test literal comprehension of the text.<br>2. Ask individual pupils to read out sentences containing I like and because, explaining their choices briefly.<br>3. Address common errors immediately and praise active participation from the entire room.<br>4. Instruct pupils to paste the completed worksheet neatly into their exercise books.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Lead a quick oral recap asking pupils to share their own favourite thing using a complete sentence with because.<br>2. Collect feedback on today's reading tasks to gauge overall confidence.<br>3. Remind pupils to review the word bank before the final session tomorrow.<br>4. Dismiss the class in an orderly manner.</p>

Reflection

The interactive whiteboard slides and information transfer worksheet engaged the stronger pupils well, but Muhammad Izzudin and Santhiya remained hesitant due to past frustrations with standard differentiation tasks. The reading text and scaffolded support helped lower-proficiency learners like Dharshayani and Nick engage meaningfully without feeling singled out by product-based changes. Overall, the vocabulary recap provided a solid foundation, though the worksheets bordered on too easy for the top tier of the class.

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1D • 2026-08-27 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.1
Session 3 of 3 favourite · colours · numbers · toy · pet · I like · because · mine

FINAL session of the 3-session unit on 'My Favourite Things'. Builds on Session 1 (Review favourite vocab -- memory game) and Session 2 (Read a short story about favourite items). Must consolidate the whole unit into a final output.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Open the interactive whiteboard slides and explicitly recap Session 1 by playing a rapid-fire memory game with the key vocabulary, including colours, numbers, toy, and pet.<br>2. Transition to Session 2 by asking pupils to recall the short story about favourite items they read previously, using the target sentence frames I like and because.<br>3. Introduce today's final writing goal, explaining that pupils will combine these elements to create a mini-profile about their favourite things.<br>4. Provide low-proficiency A0 to A1 learners with a matching card set to pair words with pictures before the main task begins.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Interactive Word Web and Sentence Building</strong></p>
<p>1. Display an interactive digital mind map on the whiteboard centered on the theme My Favourite Things.<br>2. Invite pupils to volunteer words from the word bank, specifically favourite, toy, pet, and mine, adding them to the branches.<br>3. Model how to construct a complete descriptive sentence using because for justification, writing an example on the board.<br>4. Have pupils orally practice the sentence structure with a partner to build confidence before writing.<br>5. Support A0 to A1 learners with a structured sentence frame card to ensure they can participate during the speaking check.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Guided Mini-Profile Writing Task</strong></p>
<p>1. Distribute the final consolidation writing worksheet, avoiding overly simplistic tasks by requiring pupils to write three connected sentences about their favourite things.<br>2. Instruct pupils to use at least four words from the vocabulary list, incorporating colours, numbers, and descriptive reasons.<br>3. Circulate the room to monitor progress, offering targeted spelling and grammar prompts to mid-level learners.<br>4. Provide A0 to A1 learners with a modified gap-fill version of the worksheet containing a word bank to reduce cognitive overload and prevent public anxiety.<br>5. Have pupils paste the completed worksheet into their exercise books as a verifiable record of learning for the unit.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Conduct a quick exit ticket check where pupils hold up their favourite sentence from their mini-profile on a mini whiteboard.<br>2. Praise the class for their strong engagement with the interactive whiteboard slides throughout the week.<br>3. Remind all pupils that online practice tasks have been posted for the upcoming one-week holiday, and they must finish them during the break.</p>

Reflection

The interactive whiteboard slides kept the strong learners engaged, but Muhammad Izzudin and Santhiya A/P Subramaniam still showed reluctance when pushed past basic worksheet completion. Providing the modified gap-fill successfully protected the lower-proficiency learners from public anxiety while still ensuring a valid assessment output for the end of the unit.

Wednesday DLP — Speaking Focus

R1E • 2026-08-26 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 healthy · unhealthy · vegetables · fruit · balanced · meal · sugar · too much

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'Food and Healthy Eating'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Display a picture of a burger next to a picture of an apple on the board.<br>2. Ask pupils to shout out which one is healthy and which one is unhealthy.<br>3. Write the target vocabulary words healthy, unhealthy, vegetables, and fruit on the board and model pronunciation.<br>4. Instruct pupils to repeat the words chorally and individually to ensure basic familiarity.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>

<p><strong>Vocabulary Flashcard Chant</strong></p>
<p>1. Show flashcards for the remaining target words, namely balanced, meal, sugar, and too much.<br>2. Have higher-proficiency pupils define the words in simple terms, while lower-proficiency pupils repeat the key terms.<br>3. Conduct a quick coral-chanting drill where pupils clap once for healthy words and stamp their feet for unhealthy words.<br>4. Support A0-A1 learners by providing picture prompts next to every word on the board.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p><strong>Stand Up If You Eat It</strong></p>
<p>1. Explain the rules of a speaking game where the teacher reads out food items, such as broccoli or soda.<br>2. Instruct pupils to stand up and say the sentence aloud, for example, broccoli is healthy, or soda has too much sugar.<br>3. Monitor the room closely and assist struggling speakers with sentence frames written on the board.<br>4. Ensure boys who return late from prayers are quickly paired with supportive partners to catch up on the speaking game.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p><strong>Turn and Talk Mini Dialogue</strong></p>
<p>1. Provide pairs with a simple speaking prompt card about their favorite meal.<br>2. Guide pupils to ask and answer using the pattern, my favorite meal is, and it is healthy or unhealthy.<br>3. Walk around the classroom to listen to spoken output and correct pronunciation errors gently.<br>4. Invite two confident pairs to model their short dialogues in front of the class.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Conduct a quick exit ticket by asking each pupil to name one fruit or vegetable before they pack up.<br>2. Praise active participation, particularly highlighting early finishers who helped their peers.<br>3. Remind the class that the vocabulary learned today will be used in the next writing session.</p>

Reflection

The vocabulary introduction went reasonably well, though Huzaifah and Ammar missed the first twenty minutes due to Friday prayers and needed extra guidance to catch up. Celine and Ainnur performed the speaking tasks confidently, while lower-proficiency learners like Tueszita relied heavily on the sentence frames provided on the board. Overall, oral participation was decent once the visual flashcards and choral drills established the meaning of the new terms.

Tuesday DLP — Speaking Focus

S1M • 2026-08-25 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 tall · short · friendly · kind · shy · funny · clever · hair

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'Describing People'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Greet the class warmly and display a large picture of a well-known cartoon character on the board to establish a comfortable, low-pressure atmosphere.<br>2. Ask simple open-ended questions about the character, such as "Is this person tall or short?", allowing lower-proficiency pupils to use single-word answers or gestures.<br>3. Write the target adjectives on the board and introduce the theme of describing people for the new unit.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>

<p><strong>Vocabulary Flash and Match</strong></p>
<p>1. Distribute flashcards containing the target words tall, short, friendly, kind, shy, funny, clever, and hair to pairs of pupils.<br>2. Pronounce each word clearly and have the class repeat in a choral response to build confidence, supporting A0 to A1 learners with picture cues.<br>3. Instruct pairs to match the adjectives with corresponding picture cards taped around the room in a quick, low-stakes kinesthetic check.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p><strong>Stand Up If You Are</strong></p>
<p>1. Explain the rules of a whole-class physical response game where the teacher calls out a trait, such as friendly or shy.<br>2. Model the action by standing up and smiling for friendly, encouraging hesitant pupils to participate without pressure.<br>3. Guide pupils to make simple spoken statements using sentence frames, such as "I am kind", while ensuring distant or quiet learners are gently included in mixed-ability groups.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p><strong>Pass the Photo Speaking Circle</strong></p>
<p>1. Divide the class into small, supportive circles and hand each group a picture card of a person.<br>2. Model a basic descriptive sentence using the target vocabulary, such as "He has short hair and is funny."<br>3. Prompt each pupil to say one simple sentence about the person on the card, offering direct support and sentence stems to lower-proficiency learners before they speak.</p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Conduct a quick exit ticket activity where every pupil whispers one new word they learned today to the teacher before packing up.<br>2. Praise the class for their speaking efforts, highlighting positive participation to maintain a comfortable classroom environment.<br>3. Remind pupils that the next session will build on these descriptions with more partner speaking tasks.</p>

Reflection

The lesson went reasonably well in establishing a comfortable environment, though Dhivinesh required a seat relocation to stay focused during the speaking circle. Ahmad Harraz and Fatin Aqilah led their groups well, while lower-proficiency learners like Wu and Lee Kok Sing benefited greatly from the picture prompts and sentence frames during the final activity.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Gather the class back together and ask a few volunteers to share one interesting thing they learned about a classmate during the mingling activity.<br>2.
