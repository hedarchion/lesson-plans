Monday DLP — Speaking Focus

I1D • 2026-08-24 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 favourite · colours · numbers · toy · pet · I like · because · mine

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'My Favourite Things'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher places large colorful images representing toys, pets, and different colors at various stations around the classroom.<br>2. Pupils walk in small groups to each station to observe the images and discuss what they see with their partners.<br>3. Teacher gathers the pupils and asks them to identify the common theme, introducing the topic 'My Favourite Things'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Vocabulary Introduction and Sentence Framing</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays flashcards for the unit word bank: favourite, colours, numbers, toy, pet, I like, because, and mine.<br>2. Teacher leads a choral repetition session where pupils repeat the words multiple times with increasing speed.<br>3. Teacher introduces a simple sentence frame on the board: 'I like my [toy/pet] because it is [colour]'.<br>4. Teacher models the sentence using a picture of a blue car: 'I like my toy because it is blue'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Word Bank Board Relay</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher divides the class into two teams and lists the 8 vocabulary words on the whiteboard.<br>2. One pupil from each team races to the board when the teacher calls out a definition or shows a picture.<br>3. The first pupil to touch the correct word must use it in a simple sentence to win a point for their team.<br>4. The activity continues until all pupils have had a turn to speak and identify a word.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Gallery Walk with Peer Feedback</strong></p>
<p>1. Each pupil draws their favourite toy or pet on a piece of paper and labels it using the word bank.<br>2. Pupils tape their drawings to the wall and stand next to them as 'curators'.<br>3. Other pupils walk around, listen to the curator say 'This is mine, I like it because it is [colour]', and then leave a smiley-face sticky note as feedback.<br>4. Teacher monitors and provides sentence starters to pupils who struggle to speak.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher asks pupils to keep their drawings safe because they will be used as clues in the next lesson.<br>2. Teacher tells the class that in Session 2, they will use these words to solve a 'Secret Box' mystery, creating a curiosity hook for the next meeting.</p>

Reflection

The majority of pupils successfully named 5 words, though some struggled with the conjunction 'because'. Dharshayani A/P Paul and Nick Mikhael Bin Hendra Cipta showed great confidence during the relay race. Muslimahtul Ramadani required extra scaffolding but eventually managed to use the sentence frame correctly.

Tuesday DLP — Reading Focus

S1D • 2026-08-25 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1
Session 2 of 3 favourite · colours · numbers · toy · pet · I like · because · mine

SECOND session of the 3-session unit on 'My Favourite Things'. Builds on Session 1 (Review favourite vocab -- memory game). Must OPEN with a recap of the unit vocabulary from Session 1.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays the unit word bank on the board: favourite, colours, numbers, toy, pet, I like, because, mine.<br>2. In pairs, one pupil explains to their partner what they remember about these words from the memory game played in the previous session.<br>3. The class performs a choral repetition of the word bank to reinforce pronunciation before starting the reading task.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Identifying Main Ideas and Details</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher introduces a short story: My favourite toy is a blue car. I have two cars. I like my toy because it is fast. The blue car is mine.<br>2. Teacher explains that the main idea is the 'big picture' (the toy car) and supporting details give more information (blue, fast).<br>3. Teacher models how to find the first detail by circling the word 'blue' on the whiteboard.<br>4. Teacher points out how the word 'because' from last lesson helps explain a detail.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Think-Pair-Share with Written Output</strong></p>
<p>1. Each pair thinks about the text to find the second supporting detail and the main idea.<br>2. Pairs share their findings with each other using the sentence frame: The main idea is -- and the detail is --.<br>3. One student from each pair writes the main idea and two details into a guided graphic organiser on a worksheet.<br>4. The class reviews the answers together to ensure everyone identified the car as the main idea and its colour/speed as details.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Ranking Favourite Items</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides three new sentences about a pet, a toy, and a colour, using the unit word bank.<br>2. In small groups, pupils rank these three items from 1 to 3 based on which they think is the most interesting 'favourite' thing.<br>3. A volunteer from each group justifies their top choice to the class using the frame: I like this because --.<br>4. Teacher reminds the class how these sentences are similar to the ones they practiced in the first session.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher conducts a quick quiz where pupils give a thumbs-up if a statement is a detail from the story and a thumbs-down if it is not.<br>2. Teacher tells the class that in the next session, they will use these details to create a final 'My Favourite Things' poster, combining everything from sessions one and two.</p>

Reflection

Dharshayani A/P Paul and Muhammad Firdaus Alfandi successfully identified the main idea, showing good progress from the first session. Muslimahtul Ramadani required extra guidance with the sentence frames but managed to complete the written output.

Thursday DLP — Writing Focus

K1D • 2026-08-27 • CS 4.1 / LS 4.1.1
Session 3 of 3 favourite · colours · numbers · toy · pet · I like · because · mine

FINAL session of the 3-session unit on 'My Favourite Things'. Builds on Session 1 (Review favourite vocab -- memory game) and Session 2 (Read a short story about favourite items). Must consolidate the whole unit into a final output.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays the unit word bank (favourite, colours, numbers, toy, pet, I like, because, mine) and asks pupils to recall the story from Session 2.<br>2. Pupils participate in a 5-question mini-quiz written on the board covering vocabulary from the previous two sessions.<br>3. Pupils exchange their quiz papers and self-check the answers in pairs to reinforce the spelling of the unit words.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Gift Ranking and Justification</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays five gift options on the board: a toy car, a pet rabbit, a blue shirt, 10 stickers, and a red ball.<br>2. Teacher explains that last lesson they read about gifts, and now they must rank these items from most favourite to least favourite.<br>3. Pupils work in pairs to rank the items and justify their top choice using the frame: I like the (item) because it is (colour/number).<br>4. Teacher monitors and encourages pupils to use the word --favourite-- during their discussion.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Think-Pair-Share: The Thank-You Card</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides a thank-you card template with sentence frames to support the weaker learners.<br>2. Pupils --think-- of a gift they want to write about and --pair-- up to discuss which 5 words from the unit word bank they will include.<br>3. Pupils --share-- their ideas by writing 3-4 sentences on the template, such as: Thank you for the toy. It is my favourite. I like it because it is blue. It is mine.<br>4. Teacher assists pupils like the ones who struggle with spelling to ensure they use the word bank correctly.<br>5. Pupils decorate their cards to finalize their consolidated unit product.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Pupils complete an exit ticket by writing one thing they can now do, such as --I can write about my favourite toy.--<br>2. Teacher reminds pupils that online tasks for the holiday are posted and they MUST complete them during the break before the unit wrap-up is concluded.</p>

Reflection

Wednesday DLP — Speaking Focus

R1E • 2026-08-26 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 healthy · unhealthy · vegetables · fruit · balanced · meal · sugar · too much

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'Food and Healthy Eating'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays three zoomed-in mystery images of food items on the projector screen.<br>2. Pupils guess the items and predict if they are part of a balanced diet or contain too much sugar.<br>3. Teacher reveals the full pictures and drills the unit word bank: healthy, unhealthy, vegetables, fruit, balanced, meal, sugar, and too much.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Think-Pair-Share Interview Prep</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides a list of foods and asks pupils to think individually about which are healthy or unhealthy.<br>2. In pairs, pupils discuss their choices and write three interview questions using the unit vocabulary.<br>3. Pairs exchange their written questions with another group to check for clarity and correct usage of words like 'balanced' and 'meal'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Meal Ranking and Speaking Task</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays five different meal sets on the board ranging from fast food to home-cooked dishes.<br>2. Pairs must rank these meals from 1 to 5 based on how 'balanced' they are, providing a spoken justification for their top choice.<br>3. Volunteers stand up to ask their three prepared questions to the class, while others respond using at least 6 words from the word bank.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher reads out five statements about nutrition, and pupils respond with a thumbs-up for healthy or thumbs-down for unhealthy.<br>2. Teacher announces that in the next session, pupils will use today's vocabulary to create a healthy meal plan for a school canteen.</p>

Reflection

Thursday DLP — Reading Focus

K1E • 2026-08-27 • CS 3.1 / LS 3.1.1
Session 2 of 3 healthy · unhealthy · vegetables · fruit · balanced · meal · sugar · too much

SECOND session of the 3-session unit on 'Food and Healthy Eating'. Builds on Session 1 (Food vocab review + sorting healthy/unhealthy). Must OPEN with a recap of the unit vocabulary from Session 1.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher draws a large circle on the board labeled 'Healthy Eating' to begin a recap of the unit vocabulary from Session 1.<br>2. Students are invited to come to the board to build a word web using the words 'healthy', 'unhealthy', 'vegetables', 'fruit', 'balanced', 'meal', 'sugar', and 'too much'.<br>3. Teacher asks the class to recall how they sorted these items in the previous session to ensure everyone is ready for the reading task.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Identifying Main Ideas and Supporting Details</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays a short article: 'A balanced meal is essential for growing teenagers. You must include plenty of vegetables and fruit to get vitamins. However, many snacks contain too much sugar, which is unhealthy. Eating well helps you focus in class.'<br>2. Teacher explains that the 'main idea' is the overall message of the text, while 'supporting details' provide specific evidence or examples.<br>3. Teacher demonstrates how 'balanced meal' is the main idea, and the mention of 'vitamins' and 'sugar' are the supporting details.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Ranking Healthy Habits</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher provides a list of four habits derived from the text: eating more fruit, cutting down on sugar, having a balanced meal, and eating vegetables daily.<br>2. In small groups, students must rank these habits from 1 (most important) to 4 (least important) based on the article's message.<br>3. Each group must justify their top choice to the class, explaining at least two supporting details from the text to defend their ranking.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Pair Interview: The Healthy Eating Expert</strong></p>
<p>1. Students work in pairs to conduct a short interview where one person acts as a health expert and the other as a reporter.<br>2. The reporter asks, 'What is the main message of the article we read?' and 'Can you give me two details on how to improve my meal?'<br>3. The expert must answer using their own words while incorporating at least three words from the unit word bank, such as 'balanced' or 'sugar'.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Students complete a 3-2-1 reflection: writing 3 things they learned today, 2 questions they have about healthy food, and 1 opinion on the article.<br>2. Teacher closes the lesson by telling students that in Session 3, they will use their rankings and definitions to design a final healthy plate poster.</p>

Reflection

Ainnur Raudhah and Chai Zi Jian showed great progress in identifying main ideas, while Khayla Dhia Jannah was able to explain the supporting details of a balanced meal with high confidence. The class successfully used the unit word bank to justify their rankings during the group activity.

Tuesday DLP — Speaking Focus

S1M • 2026-08-25 • CS 2.1 / LS 2.1.1
Session 1 of 3 tall · short · friendly · kind · shy · funny · clever · hair

FIRST session of a 3-session unit on 'Describing People'. Introduces the unit vocabulary that later sessions will build on.

<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Set Induction</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Teacher displays four pixelated or partially covered images of famous characters on the board.<br>2. A pupil predicts the physical appearance or personality of the character based on the visible parts using words like tall or short.<br>3. Teacher reveals the full images and introduces the unit word bank: tall, short, friendly, kind, shy, funny, clever, and hair.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Lesson Development</strong></span></p>
<p><strong>Language Structures for Describing People</strong></p>
<p>1. Teacher explains the definitions of the unit vocabulary and models the correct pronunciation.<br>2. Teacher demonstrates how to ask questions such as -- Does he have short hair? -- or -- Is she clever? -- using the board.<br>3. The class practices repeating the questions and answers in a choral drill to ensure fluency.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Trait Ranking and Justification</strong></p>
<p>1. Each group receives a set of cards featuring the eight unit words.<br>2. The class must rank the traits from most important to least important when choosing a friend and prepare a justification.<br>3. One student from each group presents their top-ranked trait to the class, explaining why they chose words like friendly or kind over others.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Think-Pair-Share: Guess Who</strong></p>
<p>1. Each pupil thinks of a person in the school and writes three descriptive sentences using the word bank.<br>2. In pairs, one student asks at least three questions to guess the person their partner is thinking of.<br>3. Each pair must use at least six different words from the bank during their conversation to meet the learning objective.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;"><strong>Post-lesson</strong></span></p>
<p>1. Each pupil completes an exit ticket by writing a one-sentence takeaway about the most important word they learned today.<br>2. Teacher announces that in the next session, the class will use these descriptions to write a short biography for a mystery classmate.</p>

Reflection

Aayra Faiz Firman and Afif Irsyaduddin showed excellent critical thinking during the ranking task by justifying why being clever is a vital trait. Ahmad Harraz was able to independently construct three complex questions using the word bank during the speaking activity.
